## What This Guide Is

This is a **translation key**, not a governing theological document.

**These labels describe where LST currently stands, not where it promises to stay.** Its positions and the labels that happen to describe them may change when stronger biblical evidence requires a different conclusion. Readers are welcome to challenge the underlying doctrine from Scripture; the labels themselves have no authority over the text.

Living Systematic Theology does not begin with denominational or system labels and then force Scripture into them. The labels below are descriptive. They tell readers which familiar theological neighborhoods LST most closely resembles **after** its present conclusions have been reached from Scripture.

If this guide ever conflicts with the current Living Systematic Theology or Creed, the current governing documents control.
Last checked against Living Systematic Theology Version 2.6 (October 10, 2026). The October 10 refinement concerns the technical grading of the numerical unity of the divine decree, not a change of the labels described in this guide.

The basic rule is simple:

> **The label describes where LST landed. The label does not decide where LST is allowed to land.**

## Quickest Shorthand

If someone asks, "What kind of theology is LST?" the shortest useful answer is:

> **LST is a conservative evangelical Protestant theology that is broadly Reformed in salvation and providence, Baptist in church and ordinances, closest to Progressive Covenantalism / New Covenant Theology in covenant structure, non-supersessionist concerning ethnic Israel, cautious-continuationist in spiritual gifts, and deliberately noncommittal between a future millennial interval and recapitulation where Revelation 20 remains unresolved.**

An even shorter comparison is:

> **Reformed Baptist soteriology + Progressive Covenantalism, with millennial structure left open where the text does not presently settle it, and with every label held loosely enough that Scripture can overrule it.**

## At a Glance

| Area | Closest Familiar Label | Important Qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overall identity | Conservative Evangelical Protestant | LST is not organized around loyalty to a denomination, confession, or theological camp. |
| Authority | Sola Scriptura | The sixty-six-book canon is final. Creeds, traditions, theologians, and AI are subordinate witnesses and tools. |
| Method | Grammatical-historical-canonical, Christ-centered | Text, grammar, context, meaning, then theology. Later revelation may explain, extend, transform, or complete earlier revelation, but the inspired argument must establish the move. |
| Doctrine of God | Classical Christian theism | God is eternal, omniscient, immutable, sovereign, and Triune. Divine simplicity is retained as a defended synthesis rather than a direct biblical formula. Biblical language about grief, relenting, desire, judgment, and response is not emptied to protect the synthesis. |
| Trinity | Nicene / classical Trinitarian, with the Filioque unresolved | One essence in three coeternal persons who are equal in deity; that equality does not erase their personal distinctions or the ordered relations Scripture reveals among them. Eternal generation of the Son is a defended synthesis rather than a confession-level formula. Scripture says the Spirit proceeds from the Father, calls Him the Spirit of the Son and of Christ, and teaches the Son sends and pours Him out. LST does not presently judge "from the Father and the Son" over "from the Father through the Son" to be demonstrated. |
| Providence and human agency | Reformed compatibilist | God's decree is comprehensive and certain, creatures act willingly and remain responsible, and compatibilism is treated as a useful description rather than an inspired metaphysical explanation. |
| Humanity and sin | Augustinian-Reformed | Historical Adam, representative headship, moral and spiritual inability, real human responsibility, and the image of God marred but not erased. |
| Creation | Conservative creationist; creation-day duration unresolved | LST treats the duration of days one through six as not demonstrated. Ordinary-day and analogical-day readings remain argued possibilities, and no particular earth-age model organizes the theology. |
| Christology | Nicene-Chalcedonian | One divine person, two complete natures; conceived by the Holy Spirit and born of the virgin Mary; bodily resurrection, present enthronement, Prophet, Priest, and King. |
| Atonement | Reformed / particular-redemption leaning | Penal, substitutionary, priestly, propitiatory, reconciling, redemptive, covenantal, and victorious. LST defends particular saving intention while refusing to shrink universal-language texts in advance. |
| Election and grace | Calvinistic / monergistic | Election is not based on foreseen faith or works. Saving faith depends upon prior sovereign divine action. |
| Regeneration and faith | Reformed in substance, exact ordo unresolved | God acts first and regeneration is God's work, but LST does not presently claim Scripture demonstrates that regeneration logically precedes saving faith. |
| Gospel offer | Reformed free-offer theology | The gospel call is sincere and universal even though God's saving purpose cannot finally fail. |
| Justification | Classic Reformation / forensic sola fide | The forensic verdict is received through faith apart from meritorious works. LST defends that justification does not universally await water baptism as its first temporal moment, while the precise causal or sacramental relation of baptism to justification and forgiveness remains not demonstrated. LST also insists that final judgment is genuinely according to works and does not reduce those works to "evidence only." |
| Perseverance | Reformed perseverance of the saints, with strong conditional language retained | Everyone truly savingly united to Christ will finally persevere and be glorified. Persevering faith, repentance, holiness, and Spirit-enabled obedience are genuinely necessary to final salvation, while not meriting it. Warning texts such as Hebrews 10:29, John 15, Galatians 5:4, Romans 11, and 2 Peter 2 remain real pressure and are not redefined by the synthesis. |
| Covenant theology | Progressive Covenantal / New Covenant Theology-adjacent | One redemptive purpose in Christ; the Mosaic covenant does not remain the Church's covenant constitution; covenant forms must be judged from their own texts. LST does not simply collapse Israel and land promises into the Church. |
| Law and Christian obedience | New Covenant / Progressive Covenantal leaning | Christians are under the law of Christ, not Sinai as their covenant administration. LST does not assume the traditional moral/civil/ceremonial division settles every New Testament question. |
| Israel and the Church | Non-supersessionist, non-dispensational | One saving people in Christ, Jew and Gentile together, while ethnic Israel retains real covenantal significance. LST confesses future corporate saving mercy toward ethnic Israel from Romans 11 without turning that confession into a land map, political constitution, or second way of salvation. |
| Land promise | Between Progressive Covenantalism and Progressive Dispensationalism | A mapped future Abrahamic-boundary polity is not demonstrated. A narrower post-coming Israel-and-Jerusalem realization remains an argued possibility. |
| Church polity | Baptist, elder-led congregational | LST favors a plurality of elders with real congregational responsibility for membership, doctrine, discipline, and recognition of officers. |
| Baptism | Credobaptist / Baptist, with stronger baptismal language than bare memorialism | Baptism follows profession of faith, ordinarily by immersion. Scripture directly joins baptism to forgiveness, washing, union-with-Christ language, and salvation. The exact causal and temporal relation among faith, baptism, regeneration, and saving union remains unresolved. LST defends that the forensic verdict is received through faith and does not universally await water baptism as its first temporal moment, while baptism's precise causal or sacramental relation to justification and forgiveness remains not demonstrated. |
| Lord's Supper | Reformed Baptist / spiritual-communion | The Supper is not a renewed sacrifice and not bare memorialism. Believers truly commune with the living Christ by the Spirit through faith. |
| Spiritual gifts | Cautious continuationist with closed canon | Foundational apostleship has ceased and no claimed revelation stands beside Scripture. The pre-Parousia cessation of tongues, prophecy, and healing is not demonstrated. First Corinthians 13, Acts 2, and 1 Corinthians 1 give real continuationist pressure, without proving uninterrupted availability of every disputed gift in every congregation or era. |
| Millennium | Millennial structure unresolved | A future millennial interval and recapitulation are both argued possibilities. LST confesses Christ's reign, return, bodily resurrection, final judgment, defeat of evil, and new creation without treating Revelation 20 as a complete dispensational program. |
| Rapture / gathering timing | Undecided; pretribulation only an argued possibility | The gathering of the saints is confessed. Revelation 3:10 supplies genuine pretribulational support, while 2 Thessalonians 1-2, Matthew 24, and related texts supply substantial counter-pressure. The exact timing is not made a test of orthodoxy or salvation. |
| Ezekiel 40-48 | Typological / Christocentric, non-dispensational | LST rejects a future guilt-removing sacrificial system after Calvary and does not call Ezekiel's sacrifices "memorial" unless Scripture establishes that claim. |
| Hell | Final punishment certain; duration/mode unresolved | LST confesses final irreversible judgment and real conscious suffering in judgment. Eternal conscious punishment and conditional immortality are both argued possibilities; neither is presently demonstrated. Matthew 25 and Revelation 14 and 20 provide strong pressure toward everlasting conscious punishment, while destruction, second-death, eternal-destruction, and corpse imagery provide real counter-pressure. |
| Final state | Historic Christian new-creation theology | Bodily resurrection, final judgment, renewed creation, and God dwelling with His people. The Christian hope is not escape from creation but its consummated renewal. |

## What the Labels Do Not Mean

These labels are not a package deal.

LST may agree strongly with a tradition in one area and reject one of its characteristic conclusions in another. It may also share a conclusion with a system while rejecting the argument that system commonly uses to reach it.

So when LST is called "Reformed," "Baptist," "Progressive Covenantal," "continuationist," or "historic premillennial," that does **not** mean:

- every doctrine normally associated with that label is accepted;
- the label has interpretive authority;
- disagreement with the label requires LST to move toward a middle position;
- or a traditional formulation is protected from biblical challenge.

The question remains the same in every locus:

> **What did the inspired author intend, and does the whole counsel of Scripture sustain the conclusion?**

## Why This Guide Exists

Labels are useful because they give readers an immediate map.

They are dangerous only when the map is mistaken for the territory.

This guide exists so a new reader can understand LST's theological location at a glance without requiring the full Systematic to become a catalog of denominational comparisons.

For the actual doctrine, arguments, qualifications, confidence levels, and unresolved questions, read the current [Living Systematic Theology](LIVING_SYSTEMATIC_THEOLOGY.md) and [LST Creed](LST_CREED.md).

If you believe one of these labels misdescribes the actual LST position, or you believe the underlying position is biblically wrong, open an Issue and choose **Challenge an LST Position**.
