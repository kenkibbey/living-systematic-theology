# Living Systematic Theology in Five Minutes

*A short introduction for readers who are new to LST*

**The short version:** Living Systematic Theology (LST) is a Christian theology that is meant to be corrected whenever Scripture requires it. The Bible is the authority; LST is a human attempt to understand it faithfully.

**This is where LST stands today, not necessarily where it will stay.** Its current beliefs and conclusions are the results of study so far. If you can show from Scripture that LST has misunderstood something, missed important evidence, said too much, or said too little, your challenge is welcome. The goal is not change for its own sake; it is to correct the theology whenever God's Word requires it.

This page is a **reading guide, not a new creed or an independent statement of doctrine**. If anything here seems incomplete or differs from the current [Systematic](LIVING_SYSTEMATIC_THEOLOGY.md), the Systematic states LST's current position, and Scripture is the final authority.

## 1. What makes LST different?

Christians have built theological systems for centuries. Those systems preserve real insights, but LST does not begin by deciding that Calvinism, Arminianism, Covenant Theology, Dispensationalism, or any other school must be right.

Instead, it asks: **What does Scripture actually teach, and how strongly does it teach it?**

The governing principle is simple: **Say what Scripture establishes. Do not claim that it establishes more than it does. Do not hold back something that it does establish.**

That means a conclusion may be defended, clarified, lowered in confidence, or abandoned when the biblical evidence warrants it.

## 2. What does LST currently believe?

LST's [Creed](LST_CREED.md) summarizes its teaching. Among its central confessions are the Triune God—Father, Son, and Holy Spirit; the eternal deity of Jesus Christ; His saving death and resurrection; salvation by God's grace; and the future resurrection, judgment, and new creation.

LST also develops conclusions from the combined teaching of many passages. Some are strongly defended but remain **theological syntheses**, rather than a claim that one verse says the whole thing. Examples include certain accounts of perseverance and believer's baptism.

And LST openly identifies questions it has **not settled**, including the duration of the creation days and aspects of the millennial timeline. Unresolved does not mean unimportant; it means LST will not claim certainty the evidence does not warrant.

For the qualifications and passages behind any position, read the [full Systematic](LIVING_SYSTEMATIC_THEOLOGY.md).

## 3. Why do the different confidence levels matter?

LST separates the strength of a conclusion from how strongly someone feels about it:

- **What we confess:** Biblical conclusions LST holds as established at the stated level.
- **What we defend as synthesis:** Conclusions drawn from Scripture that require reasoning across passages and remain open to biblical testing.
- **What remains unresolved:** Questions for which LST does not claim a demonstrated answer.

These are guides to the **strength of the evidence**, not a ranking of which passages matter. The [Creed](LST_CREED.md) is arranged around these distinctions. For unfamiliar words, see the [plain-language glossary](LST_GLOSSARY.md).

## 4. How should I read LST?

**Start small.** You do not need to read the entire Systematic before asking a question.

1. Read the [Creed](LST_CREED.md) for an overview.
2. Choose one subject in the [Systematic's chapter contents](LIVING_SYSTEMATIC_THEOLOGY.md#contents).
3. Check the passages, qualifications, and counterarguments for the particular claim.
4. If familiar denominational labels help you get oriented, use the [Theological Labels Guide](LST_THEOLOGICAL_LABELS_GUIDE.md)—but remember that labels do not decide LST's conclusions.

## 5. What if I think LST is wrong?

**You are invited to challenge it.** You do not have to agree with LST or be a professional theologian.

Identify the position, explain what you think Scripture establishes instead, and show the biblical evidence. LST asks challengers to explain what evidence might change their minds, too. The [short challenge instructions in the README](README.md#how-to-challenge-lst) show the five requested elements; [CONTRIBUTING](CONTRIBUTING.md#how-to-challenge-lst) explains the full public process.

You may also try the [optional official AI Auditor Prompt](LST_OFFICIAL_AI_AUDITOR_PROMPT.md) before submitting, but an AI verdict cannot prevent you from challenging LST. You need a GitHub account to open an issue.

Substantive challenges are examined in public, with the strongest biblical arguments on both sides. [The Revision Record](LST_REVISION_RECORD.md) explains significant changes. AI can help examine arguments, but it does **not** have authority to determine doctrine or approve changes.

**The aim is not to win a theological contest. It is to let Scripture correct our theology.**
