# Step 3 — What LST Believes and How Certain It Is

LST's present teaching is stated in the **Living Systematic Theology**. A shorter **Creed** summarizes its central claims and their degrees of confidence. Those are separate documents because a short introduction cannot carry every biblical argument or qualification.

Among LST's central confessions are the Triune God—Father, Son, and Holy Spirit—the eternal deity of Jesus Christ, His saving death and resurrection, salvation by God's grace, and the future resurrection, judgment, and new creation.

Other conclusions require substantial reasoning across many passages. These are sometimes **defended syntheses**: serious, biblically supported theological conclusions that should not be presented as though one verse stated their whole technical formulation. LST also records genuine **argued possibilities** and **unresolved questions**, rather than presenting them as settled doctrines.

## Why these confidence levels matter

- **Confession:** Scripture establishes the claim at the level LST states it.
- **Defended synthesis:** Several passages form a strong biblical case, but real theological inference is involved.
- **Argued possibility:** An interpretation has meaningful support but has not been demonstrated as the necessary conclusion.
- **Not demonstrated or unresolved:** Available evidence is not sufficient to settle the proposition as asserted.

These describe **how strongly the evidence warrants a particular claim**, not how much a person cares about it or how important the subject is. A single clear verse can be decisive when the contextual argument actually establishes the claim; a cumulative biblical argument can also be decisive.

To avoid confusing readers, the source documents and explanatory tools are all collected together in **Step 7**, after the review process has been explained. There you can explore the complete Systematic, Creed, shorter introduction, glossary, and theological comparisons in whatever depth you choose.
