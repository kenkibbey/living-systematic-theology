# Step 2 — Scripture and the Governing Maxim

Scripture is the final authority. LST's Systematic is the project's present theological formulation, not another rule alongside the Bible. The Creed summarizes the Systematic; it does not replace it. Historic confessions and scholarship can provide evidence, but they cannot determine in advance what a passage must say.

## The governing maxim

> **Living Systematic Theology seeks to state the theology of Scripture as nearly as fallible interpreters can, speaking with the certainty Scripture gives, refusing certainty Scripture does not give, adding nothing the text cannot sustain, and surrendering nothing the text requires us to confess.**

Where Scripture speaks clearly, LST should speak clearly. Where Scripture does not settle a question, LST should not pretend it has. The goal is not caution for its own sake or compromise between theological camps. The goal is **faithfulness**.

This governing principle has two sides. We must not add certainty that the evidence does not support. We must also not weaken something Scripture clearly establishes simply because other readers disagree. LST can therefore **retain, clarify, strengthen, qualify, lower or raise confidence in, or abandon** a formulation as the evidence requires.

A real doctrine should survive more than the absence of an objection: it should have positive biblical warrant. An objection should also prove more than the possibility of a rival reading before replacing an established claim. That is the basis of careful correction rather than uncritically collecting conflicting doctrines.

The grammatical, historical, literary, and canonical interpretive controls will be explained in detail when you reach the four-AI review guide in Step 6. They are methods for reading Scripture carefully, not sources of authority independent of Scripture.
