# Chapter 3 — The Triune God

*Generated reading copy from LST Version **2.7**. The [complete current Systematic](../../LIVING_SYSTEMATIC_THEOLOGY.md) governs doctrine; this page must not be edited independently.*

There is one living and true God, eternal, self-existent, holy, righteous, wise, good, loving, sovereign, and without limit in His knowledge or power (Deuteronomy 6:4; Psalm 90:2; Isaiah 46:9-10). The Father is God, the Son is God, and the Holy Spirit is God; they are personally distinct without being separate gods (John 1:1-18; 20:28; Acts 5:3-4; Matthew 28:19; 2 Corinthians 13:14). LST confesses one divine essence in three coeternal persons who are equal in deity. This equality of deity does not erase their personal distinctions or the ordered relations Scripture reveals among them. Their personal distinctions are eternal: the Father loved the Son and the Son shared His glory before creation; all things are from the Father and through the Son (John 17:5, 24; 1 Corinthians 8:6). In redemption, the Father sends the Son, who comes from the Father into the world, receives and accomplishes His mediatorial commission, and as the risen Mediator calls the Father His God (John 5:19-30; 14:28; 16:28; 20:17; Matthew 28:18). At the consummation the incarnate Son will be subject to the Father; His kingdom has no end, and the new creation contains the throne of God and the Lamb (1 Corinthians 15:24-28; Luke 1:33; Revelation 22:1-3). These texts establish real personal and mediatorial order, but they do not by themselves establish inequality of divine nature or an eternal hierarchy of command and obedience between the divine persons as such. LST therefore uses equality language to confess equality of deity, not interchangeability of persons, identical personal relations, or the absence of revealed order between Father and Son. The Son is eternal God, uncreated, eternally the Son of the Father, and uniquely related to Him. LST defends eternal generation as the strongest synthesis of that relation: the Father eternally begets the Son, not as an act of creation or a beginning in time, but as an eternal relation of origin. John presents the unique Son as eternally toward the Father, says that as the Father has life in Himself so He has granted the Son also to have life in Himself, records the glory and love shared before the foundation of the world, and Hebrews calls the Son the radiance of God's glory and exact imprint of His nature (John 1:1-3, 14, 18; 5:26; 17:5, 24; Hebrews 1:3). These texts strongly ground eternal generation, but no verse states the technical proposition in those exact terms. LST therefore holds eternal generation as a defended synthesis rather than a confession-level formula. Jesus says that the Spirit "proceeds from the Father" (John 15:26). Scripture also calls Him the Spirit of the Son and the Spirit of Christ and teaches that the Son sends Him from the Father and, exalted at God's right hand, receives the promised Spirit from the Father and pours Him out (Galatians 4:6; Romans 8:9; John 15:26; 16:7; Acts 2:33). These texts establish a real relation of the Spirit to both Father and Son. They do not state that the Spirit's eternal origin is "from the Father and the Son" rather than "from the Father through the Son," and they do not by themselves settle whether John 15:26 is speaking directly of eternal origin or of the Spirit's mission. LST therefore no longer confesses the Western Filioque formula as a demonstrated biblical proposition. The exact eternal relation of the Spirit to the Son beyond what Scripture states is not demonstrated.

God does not acquire knowledge, discover circumstances, or revise His eternal purpose. His character does not change (Malachi 3:6; James 1:17). Scripture directly establishes that God is one, is spirit, needs nothing, and is unchanging in character and purpose (Deuteronomy 6:4; John 4:24; Acts 17:24-25; Malachi 3:6; James 1:17). LST defends divine simplicity as a systematic synthesis from these and related truths: God's being is not composed of separable parts, and His attributes are not competing psychological faculties. Scripture does not state that technical formulation in those exact terms. The synthesis must therefore remain governed by the full biblical portrayal of God and must not be employed to make His revealed love, anger, grief, mercy, and personal address empty words.

Scripture depicts God as testing Abraham, relenting in judgment, responding to prayer, grieving over rebellion, and calling the wicked to turn and live (Genesis 22:1-12; Jeremiah 18:7-10; Ezekiel 18:23, 32; 33:11; Matthew 23:37). These are truthful disclosures of God's dealings with actual persons in history. In Genesis 22 the test genuinely proves Abraham within the unfolding covenant relationship; it does not supply information previously missing from God's omniscience. The creature's circumstances and standing change. God's holiness, wisdom, and eternal purpose remain constant, while His dealings with the creature take the form appropriate to those changed circumstances.

God's righteous opposition to evil is genuine. His invitation to repent is sincere. He is not a God who delights in wickedness (Psalm 5:4), and He swears that He has no pleasure in the death of the wicked but in the wicked turning and living (Ezekiel 18:23, 32; 33:11). At the same time, no event defeats His decree. The crucifixion demonstrates that God can ordain an event for a holy purpose while condemning the wicked intentions of its human agents (Acts 2:23; 4:27-28). God's eternal purpose and His real historical address must both be confessed. Scripture does not disclose an exhaustive account of how divine eternity, simplicity, and temporal interaction meet within God's life; the absence of that account does not authorize denying either side of the biblical witness.
