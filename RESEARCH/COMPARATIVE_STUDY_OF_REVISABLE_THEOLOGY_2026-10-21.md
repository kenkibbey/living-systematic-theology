# How Distinctive Is Living Systematic Theology?
## A Source-Checked Comparison of Revisable Christian Theology and Public Review Practices

**Research note · October 21, 2026 · LST baseline: Version 2.9 / Revision 50**  
**Status: Non-governing, AI-assisted comparative research; not independent academic peer review**  
**Prepared for:** Living Systematic Theology (LST)  
**Scope:** Operational and methodological similarities, **not** a ranking of which Christian theology is true.

> **Finding:** LST is not the first theology to be openly revisable, to invite criticism, or to preserve historical doctrinal changes. What appears **unusual among the documented comparisons** is the combination of a continuously maintained systematic, differentiated confidence claims, a formal resistance test before revision, a public challenge and rebuttal trail, archived superseded formulations, and software-like versioning. The public four-AI role system is **a pilot, not adopted standing policy**. This research did **not** establish that LST is unique in the world.

## 1. Why this comparison matters

The test is not whether other Christians have said *semper reformanda* or issued second editions of theological books. The question is whether another identifiable **working system** combines the following features: (a) broad doctrinal scope, (b) correction by a declared primary authority, (c) explicit confidence distinctions, (d) a repeatable process that gives both challengers and incumbents their strongest case, (e) visible revision history with a reason for each consequential change, (f) current working text distinguishable from superseded views, and (g) public avenues for documented challenges.

A denomination with extensive parliamentary revision rules may be institutionally more accountable than LST but may not produce one continuously updated systematic theology. A digital research project may have unusually careful sourcing labels but may **describe other people's doctrines**, not advance and revise its own coherent theology. These distinctions prevent false equivalence.

## 2. LST's documented method, not an idealized one

The current [Systematic](../LIVING_SYSTEMATIC_THEOLOGY.md), [Creed](../LST_CREED.md), [Revision Record](../LST_REVISION_RECORD.md), [public challenge rules](../CONTRIBUTING.md), and [review pilot guide](../REVIEW/README.md) provide the controlling self-description.

- **A revisable doctrinal text:** The present Systematic is Version **2.9**, with an explicit biblical governing maxim. The Creed is a subordinate summary, **not** absent or replaced by a review board.
- **Graded claims:** The theology distinguishes confession, defended synthesis, argued possibility and unresolved or unproved matters. These are epistemic judgments, not votes or numeric probabilities.
- **Revision Resistance:** Before altering a claim, the reviewer must construct the strongest biblical case for **retaining** it, then fairly test the opposing case. An unsupported proof text is to be repaired where the doctrine itself survives; a scripturally defeated claim is to be corrected.
- **Change control:** Significant changes go into a Revision Record; prior governing states remain archived in *Old Systematics* in the continuing editable Drive repository. The public GitHub release has its own separately checked version history. Minor refinements and genuine doctrinal reversals are distinguished by versioning policy.
- **Public criticism:** Contributors can submit challenges; official adjudications must disclose arguments, objections, reasons, and rebuttal opportunities. The current working editor/owner authorizes changes. There is **no independent ecclesial legislature or automatic majority-vote mechanism**.
- **Experimental AI assistance:** Public Prompts A/B/C/D divide challenger advocacy, defender Revision Resistance, provisional adjudication and source verification, but **the four-AI structure remains an unadopted pilot**. Its reports are not independent human peer review; separation of chats does not guarantee independence; the same model has sometimes served two roles. Do not present “AI consensus” as biblical authority.

A concrete illustration is [Issue #35](https://github.com/kenkibbey/living-systematic-theology/issues/35): examination of eternal conscious punishment produced **no confidence promotion**, but did produce Version 2.9 / Revision 50 passage-level clarifications after public, preserved role reports. The original report, its corrections and the challenge's public history remain inspectable. That is stronger evidence for functioning revision than a merely aspirational statement that the author is open-minded.

## 3. Strong comparisons

### A. Andrew Perriman's *Open Source Theology* and later P.OST — closest **philosophical/process metaphor**

Andrew Perriman states that he ran a collaborative site called **Open Source Theology** around **2002–2009**, then moved to a personal, narrative-historical theology site, P.OST. It represented a real earlier attempt to make biblical and doctrinal rethinking more open and dialogical. See [Perriman's own retrospective](https://www.postost.net/2016/05/who-founder-narrative-historical-hermeneutic) (March 2016) and his [statement of method](https://www.postost.net/2011/08/towards-useful-narrative-historical-theology) (August 2011).

**Parallel:** Theology should be open to biblical reinterpretation and communal argument rather than merely reproducing closed packages. **Difference:** The source examined does not establish a currently maintained comprehensive Systematic with LST's specific confidence categories, Git-based releases, challenge verdicts and doctrine-change register. *Open Source Theology* is therefore a major **precedent**, not evidence that LST invented the open-theology idea.

### B. *Examined Doctrine* — closest discovered **digital evidence-labeling analogue**

The [methodology page, edition 2026-08](https://examineddoctrine.com/method/), describes a maintained digital comparison of theologians and confessions with **per-claim sourcing tiers**: researched from a primary source, established but not rechecked, contested, and unknown. It differentiates what a thinker actually said from editorial derivation; it makes corrections visible, acknowledges thin coverage, and reports AI assistance. It even labels deliberate nonpositions instead of treating every blank cell as ignorance.

**Parallel:** Publicly explicit evidential status, traceable sourcing, honest uncertainty, correction notices, and digital editions. **Difference:** It maps **other thinkers' positions** rather than serving as a comprehensive, internally integrated systematic theology holding its *own* graduated biblical conclusions. Its tiers grade **source verification**, while LST grades **doctrinal warrant**. This is a substantive nearby analogue and an instructive model for LST.

### C. *The Theology of Work Project* — closest documented **multidisciplinary editorial-review model**

The [project's own editorial account](https://www.theologyofwork.org/about) says its biblical commentary and key-topic articles undergo review by **at least 15–20 expert reviewers** and final steering-committee approval; contributors span scholars, pastors, ministry leaders, and workplace Christians. It also invites reader feedback on its articles.

**Parallel:** Biblical argumentation, deliberate revision before publication, multiple perspectives and feedback. **Difference:** The project is a **work-focused commentary and resource body**, not one open-ended systematic doctrine under changing confidence grades. Its substantial **human review** is a strength that LST's AI pilot does **not** replace. This comparison cautions against confusing number of AI roles with number of independent specialists.

### D. Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.) *Book of Confessions* — closest **formal confessional-change precedent**

The [PC(USA) constitutional resources](https://pcusa.org/about-pcusa/agencies-entities/life-witness/ministry-areas/constitutional-interpretation/constitution) identify the *Book of Confessions* as Part I of the denomination's constitution. The [2016 addition of the Confession of Belhar](https://pcusa.org/news-storytelling/news/2016/6/23/belhar-added-pcusas-book-confessions) followed a multi-year study and voting process. An earlier effort **failed** because it did not reach the necessary two-thirds presbytery threshold; the renewed effort succeeded. The [official explanation of the process](https://pcusa.org/news-storytelling/news/2016/6/21/panel-approves-addition-belhar-pcusa-book-confessions) describes committee, General Assembly and presbytery stages.

**Parallel:** Confessions are not historically motionless; changes are studied, publicly justified, ratified and documented, including unsuccessful proposals. **Difference:** Its governance involves actual church courts/assemblies and representative ratification, unlike LST's owner-maintained theological project. The Book is a **collection of historical confessions** rather than a single prose systematic rewritten issue by issue. Neither model should claim to possess the other's kind of accountability.

### E. United Methodist Church *Social Principles* — strongest documented **living teaching document with broad consultation**

The [official preface](https://www.umc.org/en/content/preface-social-principles) calls the Social Principles a **living document**, records repeated revisions, describes wide consultations, and states that they are not church law. The extensive revision effort initiated in 2012 resulted in the [adoption of the Revised Social Principles in April–May 2024](https://www.umcjustice.org/latest/done-all-sections-of-the-revised-social-principles-officially-adopted-1945), with participation from thousands across the world.

**Parallel:** An identifiable, revisable official Christian teaching text, extensive consultation, reasoned amendment, and documented adoption. **Difference:** It is a **denominational social-ethical teaching document**, not a comprehensive systematic theology arranged by epistemic confidence; its governance is by an elected General Conference, unlike LST. UMC's participation breadth should be credited, not scored below LST simply because the text is not on GitHub.

### F. Evangelical Lutheran Church in America *Social Statements* — strongest documented **graded institutional adoption procedure**

The [ELCA's official explanation](https://www.elca.org/about-the-elca/who-we-are/our-purpose/social-teachings) describes consultation and deliberation for broad social statements, which require a **two-thirds Churchwide Assembly vote**. Its [2025 civic-life statement](https://www.elca.org/social-teachings/2025-civic-life-theological-discernment-social-statements) illustrates a contemporary approved addition.

**Parallel:** Transparent, revisable formal teaching and a published decision rule. **Difference:** Assembly approval concerns **institutional legitimacy**, whereas LST confidence grades concern an editor's assessment of **what Scripture warrants**. A two-thirds vote is *not* analogous to a theological proof threshold; conflating them obscures how both operate.

### G. Southern Baptist Convention *Baptist Faith and Message* — clear **nonfinal confession with real revisions**

The SBC's [official comparison of 1925, 1963 and 2000](https://bfm.sbc.net/) and its [2023 retrospective statement](https://www.sbc.net/resource-library/resolutions/on-the-southern-baptist-confessional-heritage-of-the-office-of-bishop-elder-pastor/) recognize that Baptist confessions are not exhaustive, final or infallible expressions of faith and have been revised.

**Parallel:** An explicitly fallible doctrinal confession revisited over generations in response to questions. **Difference:** It is a relatively stable confessional statement periodically amended by a convention, not a continually maintained multi-chapter systematic with issue-level, evidence-by-evidence public change accounting. A revision process exists even where frequent Git-based releases do not.

### H. Presbyterian Church in America *Book of Church Order* — useful **version-control contrast**, not a direct doctrinal rival

The PCA's [official Book of Church Order page](https://www.pcaac.org/bco/) exposes current editions and an **amendment-tracking dashboard**, while stating its constitution is subordinate to Scripture. This demonstrates that Christian institutions can track proposed revisions and preserve explicit rules.

**Parallel:** Ongoing maintenance of accountable formal texts with visible amendment tracking. **Difference:** It primarily governs **polity and discipline**, not an open systematic graded by biblical warrant. It would be misleading to claim that LST alone has public change tracking.

**How to read the table:** “Not shown” means the examined sources do not demonstrate the feature; it is **not proof of universal absence**. A denomination's formal governance and larger human deliberation are not automatically less accountable because LST has granular GitHub history.

## 5. What appears distinctive—and what does not

**Well supported by LST's own observable records:** The combination of a broad, maintained theological Systematic; declared confidence distinctions; Revision Resistance before changing an incumbent formulation; separation of argument repair from doctrinal reversal; public issue-by-issue reasons; preservation of superseded governing states; and minor-versus-major semantic versioning. Issue #35 shows several of these features actually operating.

**Precedented elsewhere:** Being willing to revise, centering Scripture, welcoming readers' objections, involving multiple reviewers, preserving older editions, recording a rejected challenge, and publishing source limits. None of these alone was invented by LST.

**Not demonstrated here:** “No other theology in history works this way,” a worldwide ranking for openness, a first-ever AI theology, automatic independence of AI roles, academic peer review, or universal access to every internal working source. A finite comparative survey cannot prove the absence of all unknown analogues.

**Best-supported description:** *LST is a distinctive current combination of established principles and digital practices; among the documented systems compared here, no exact operational counterpart was verified.* This is a bounded comparative finding, **not** a claim of proven historical uniqueness.

## 6. Methodological lessons for LST

1. **Learn from Examined Doctrine:** Label *source-check status* separately from LST's *doctrinal-confidence grade*. A passage can support a strong inference while its secondary scholarly evidence has not been checked, or vice versa.
2. **Learn from Theology of Work:** Seek independent specialists and pastoral voices where possible. A four-AI pilot is no substitute for qualified human review or independent Greek manuscript examination.
3. **Learn from PC(USA):** Publish a clear account of which authority adopts decisions and preserve failed as well as successful proposed revisions.
4. **Learn from UMC/ELCA:** Broaden external participation without converting biblical truth into a majority vote. Explain who may contribute, who decides, and what recourse critics have.
5. **Protect LST's strongest feature:** Preserve adversarial **retention** arguments before accepting changes; do not use criticism as an automatic verdict or treat agreement between AIs as proof.
6. **Do not overstate transparency:** Distinguish public documents, private Drive working records, and unavailable historical audits. Publish the verified source trail and explicitly flag unexamined material.

These are research recommendations only. **They do not authorize an LST doctrine, confidence, governance, or four-AI-policy change.**

## 7. Method and limitations of this research note

This study was developed from an initial **AI Deep Research draft**, then corrected against current LST primary materials and the linked public external sources. The initial draft contained material overstatements, so it was **not reproduced as an endorsed public research result**:

- It assigned LST a **2021 origin**, which was not verified; the timeline is omitted here.
- It incorrectly said LST had **“no fixed creed”** and implied an actual editorial board and reviewer-consensus rule. LST has a current subordinated Creed; the owner retains decision authority; the four-AI system is a **pilot**.
- It offered subjective **0–10 openness scores** as if measurements; they have been removed.
- It tended to treat denominational ethics documents as equivalent to a full systematic theology; this report flags that category difference.
- It suggested all LST evidence and drafts were public. The Drive working repository and some historical materials are not necessarily publicly accessible.
- It leaned on Wikipedia for PC(USA)'s confessional changes; the present study uses the denomination's **own 2016 documentation** instead.
- It assumed a high reviewer count or denominational vote can be mapped to LST's doctrinal certainty; that is an analogy, not a shared standard.

**Research limits:** This is a **targeted and non-exhaustive** public-source comparison, not a census of all theological works, historic editions, privately circulated seminaries' statement-of-faith revisions, open-source repositories or non-English projects. It did not independently interview any organization, perform a bibliometric census, or inspect every draft and voting record. Claims about other organizations rest mainly on **official self-descriptions**, supplemented by directly observable public pages. The finding of “no exact counterpart verified among these examples” has **moderate confidence**; an absolute uniqueness claim has **insufficient evidence**.

**Independence and provenance:** Written with AI research assistance for the LST editor; it is not a finding by unaffiliated theologians or an independent academic panel. The author/editor can update the **research note** as new counterexamples arise. Current doctrine and official method continue to be controlled only by the governing LST documents.
