## REVISION 28
GitHub Issue #22 — Trinitarian equality, Father-Son order, and public challenge adjudication

Issue #22, submitted by Harry Farrell with his paper *24 Reasons the Trinity Doctrine is not Biblical*, was subjected to full Revision Resistance. The paper's cumulative "Priority Model" was evaluated rather than only its abbreviated issue statement.

**Case against revision.** The strongest case for retaining LST's Trinitarian confession remains substantial. John presents the Word as God, existing in the beginning, and as the one through whom all created things came to be (John 1:1-3), records the Son's pre-world glory and the risen Jesus addressed as "my Lord and my God" (John 17:5; 20:28), and Hebrews applies divine address and Psalm 102's Creator language to the Son (Hebrews 1:8-12). Philippians 2:6-11 culminates in Isaiah 45's universal homage being rendered to Jesus Christ as Lord. The Spirit speaks, wills, teaches, testifies, guides, can be grieved and lied to, and searches even the depths of God (John 14:26; 15:26; 16:13-14; Acts 5:3-4; 13:2; 1 Corinthians 2:10-11; 12:11; Ephesians 4:30). Farrell's model does not adequately account for this cumulative evidence.

**What the challenge established.** Farrell correctly pressed texts that establish genuine Father-Son distinction and order: the Father sends the Son; gives authority, judgment, life-in-Himself, and the mediatorial kingdom to the Son; is called the God and Father of Jesus; and is excepted when all things are subjected to Christ. First Corinthians 15:24-28 reaches beyond Christ's earthly humiliation and says that in the consummation the Son Himself will be subject to the One who subjected all things to Him. LST's unqualified phrase "three coequal, coeternal persons" could be heard as denying or flattening those revealed relations. Scripture does not permit that flattening.

**What the challenge did not establish.** Personal distinction, reception, mission, subjection, image-language, genuine humanity, death, temptation, or relation of origin do not by themselves demonstrate inequality of divine nature, createdness, or a temporal beginning of the Son. The argument that Luke 10:22 excludes the Spirit from divine knowledge conflicts especially with 1 Corinthians 2:10-11. Grammatical neuter gender for *pneuma* does not establish impersonality. Being called *theos* by itself is also insufficient to establish identity with the one true God; LST therefore rejects that simplistic proof while retaining the larger canonical case for the Son's deity.

**What Version 2.4 says instead.** LST continues to confess one divine essence, the full eternal deity of Father, Son, and Spirit, the Son's uncreated eternal Sonship, and the personal deity of the Spirit. Chapter 3 and the Creed now say that the three coeternal persons are equal in deity and explicitly distinguish equality of divine nature from interchangeability of persons or absence of revealed order. The Father sends the Son; the Son is from the Father, receives and accomplishes His mediatorial commission from the Father, calls the Father His God, and in the consummation the incarnate Son remains subject to the Father. These establish real personal and mediatorial order without by themselves establishing inequality of divine nature.

**Verdict:** PARTIALLY SUSTAINED. The challenge materially qualifies LST's wording of Trinitarian equality but does not overturn the Trinity, the Son's eternal deity/uncreatedness, or the personal deity of the Spirit. This is a minor release because the positive doctrine is retained with a material qualification rather than reversed or abandoned.

**Public challenge procedure.** This issue also exposed a methodological gap. A public challenger must be able to see why a challenge was sustained, partially sustained, or not sustained and must be able to test LST's adjudication. T.A.I.T. Prompt and Engagement Module now require a substantive, self-contained adjudication on the same GitHub issue before closure, including concessions, controlling biblical reasons, resulting changes, and an invitation to rebut the adjudication. A rebuttal remains in the same issue unless it is genuinely a new challenge. Closure records the present adjudication; it does not immunize LST from renewed examination.

## VERSIONING AND STABLE-FILENAME ARCHITECTURE

October 7, 2026

Repository and workflow architecture only. No LST doctrine changed and the current Systematic remains Version 1.11.

Only the Living Systematic Theology itself now carries the project's active semantic version number. The current governing filenames are stable: LIVING_SYSTEMATIC_THEOLOGY.md, LST_CREED.md, ENGAGEMENT_MODULE_LST.txt, TAIT_PROMPT_LST.txt, 00_LST_BOOTSTRAP_CURRENT.txt, and LST_REVISION_RECORD.txt. The version stated inside LIVING_SYSTEMATIC_THEOLOGY.md identifies the current theological release. Supporting governing documents no longer receive independent semantic version numbers and no longer hard-code a particular Systematic version. The Creed now says that it summarizes the current Living Systematic Theology. The T.A.I.T. prompt and bootstrap point to stable current filenames.

Major/minor rule. Minor increments within the current major line record material changes that refine but do not reverse an affirmatively held doctrine: clarification, qualification, evidence reweighting, confidence adjustment, new counter-texts, changed unresolved status, methodological refinement, or comparable correction. A genuine doctrinal overturn is different. When Scripture requires LST to reverse or abandon in substance an affirmatively held doctrine, the next Systematic release advances to the next major number and resets the minor number to zero. Thus, after Version 1.11, the first genuine doctrinal overturn will produce Version 2.0. Later non-overturning revisions will proceed 2.1, 2.2, and so on; another genuine doctrinal overturn will require Version 3.0. The origin of the correction does not matter: a GitHub challenge, internal audit, or other serious examination is judged by the same rule.

An affirmatively held doctrine for this purpose includes a positive LST conclusion held as confession or defended synthesis. Moving such a doctrine to its contradiction, abandoning it, or reducing it to not demonstrated because its positive claim cannot be sustained counts as a doctrinal overturn. Refining its scope, improving its proof, adding qualifications, changing an argued possibility, or adjusting an unresolved question without reversing the positive doctrine does not.

History preservation. Stable current filenames do not eliminate archival versioning. Before a stable current governing file is materially changed, the superseded snapshot is preserved in Old Systematics under a historical filename tied to the relevant Systematic version, Revision Record entry, or date. The Git history and Revision Record preserve the public development. This prevents stale cross-document references while retaining a complete theological and methodological history.

Effect: future LST revisions normally require changing the semantic version in one governing document only, while the rest of the current operating set continues to point to stable filenames and to the current Systematic rather than to hard-coded version numbers.

### ISSUE #11 - SUSTAINED - REVELATION 20:4-6 AND THE MILLENNIAL INTERVAL

What stood in Version 2.0. LST held a future millennial interval before the final judgment as a defended synthesis and called the grammar of Revelation 20:4-6 its strongest positive ground. The same verb for coming to life governs the faithful and the rest of the dead, and LST ranked that local feature in favor of the consecutive reading.

**Case against revision.** The consecutive reading retains substantial local force. The same verb in the same form governs both groups; "the rest of the dead" naturally places both groups among the dead; "until" reads naturally as delayed counterpart; John calls the first event a resurrection; and Revelation 2:8 and 13:14 use the same verb for return to life after death. The normal New Testament use of resurrection language is bodily. These facts prevent treating recapitulation as an easy or demonstrated solution.

Why the former grade could not stand. The earlier ranking had not weighed several local Johannine features. John pairs a first resurrection with a second death, and the second death is the lake of fire rather than ordinary physical death. The subjects of 20:4 are "souls," echoing 6:9. Revelation 14:13 blesses the dead who die in the Lord, while 20:6 blesses those who share the first resurrection. John 5:24-29 and 11:24-25 show life language standing beside future bodily resurrection, and Revelation 21:1-4 uses "first" for the passing order. None proves recapitulation, but together they make the local feature genuinely contested. Since the structural, battle/Gog, and single-end evidence already supplied substantial recapitulation pressure, LST no longer has adequate grounds to call the interval a defended synthesis.

What Version 2.1 says instead. A future millennial interval and recapitulation are both argued possibilities. The shared certainties remain: Christ reigns, He will return, the dead will rise, the last revolt fails, judgment comes, and the new creation is the goal. Revelation 20 does not independently provide a territorial map, restored cultus, festival calendar, or civil order.

### ISSUE #12 - SUSTAINED - THE DURATION OF THE CREATION DAYS

What stood in Version 2.0. Chapter 5 "presently favored" the ordinary-day understanding from numbered days, evening and morning, and Exodus 20:11 while admitting that the seventh day and alternate temporal accounts still deserved direct textual consideration. "Presently favors" was not one of LST's confidence categories.

**Case against revision.** The ordinary-day reading has real positive evidence: numbered days, the evening-and-morning refrain for days one through six, the explicit Sabbath pattern in Exodus 20:11, and Mark 10:6. These prevent dismissing the reading as merely traditional.

Why the lean could not stand. Genesis 2:2-3 gives the seventh day no evening-and-morning closure. Hebrews 4:3-10 uses God's seventh-day rest as a reality still open to be entered "today." Exodus 31:17 says God rested and was refreshed, language that cannot imply divine weariness and therefore shows analogy in the creation-week/Sabbath correspondence. Genesis 2:4 also demonstrates flexibility in day language at the seam of the accounts. The file therefore contains substantial evidence in both directions, and LST had graded a lean before working the counter-file.

What Version 2.1 says instead. The duration of days one through six is not demonstrated. Ordinary-day and analogical-day readings remain argued possibilities. The central doctrines of creation, historical Adam and Eve, the fall, image-bearing humanity, and redemption in Christ do not depend on settling the duration.

### ISSUE #13 - SUSTAINED - THE IMPUTATION FORMULA

What stood in Version 2.0. Chapter 9 said without qualification, "Christ's righteousness is reckoned to the believer."

**Case against revision.** Romans 5:18-19 strongly grounds the believer's righteous standing in Christ's obedience; 2 Corinthians 5:21, Philippians 3:9, and 1 Corinthians 1:30 add converging support. The doctrine therefore has a substantial biblical case and need not be abandoned.

Why the wording required correction. Paul's explicit reckoning sentences in Romans 4 say that faith is counted as righteousness and that God counts righteousness apart from works. They do not contain the exact formula that Christ's own righteousness is what is reckoned. Moving from Christ's obedience as the ground and believers being constituted righteous to the technical imputation formula is a theological synthesis.

What Version 2.1 says instead. LST retains the imputation conclusion as a defended synthesis and marks it as such. Justification remains forensic, grounded solely in Christ's obedience and blood, received through faith apart from works, and no human obedience contributes to its meritorious ground.

### ISSUE #7 - SUSTAINED - THE FILIOQUE AND THE SPIRIT'S PROCESSION

What stood in Version 1.11. Chapter 3 and the Creed confessed that the Spirit proceeds eternally "from the Father and the Son." The Systematic acknowledged that the technical form was not a verbatim scriptural formula but still treated the content as confessional doctrine.

**Case against revision.** The strongest biblical defense comes from the Spirit's deep relation to the Son. Scripture calls Him the Spirit of the Son and the Spirit of Christ (Galatians 4:6; Romans 8:9), says the Son sends Him from the Father (John 15:26; 16:7), records the exalted Son receiving the promised Spirit from the Father and pouring Him out (Acts 2:33), and says the Spirit takes what belongs to the Son and declares it (John 16:14-15). These texts rule out treating the Spirit's relation to the Son as merely external or accidental and provide substantial grounds for a Western theological inference.

Why the confession could not stand. John 15:26 is the only New Testament sentence that directly uses procession language of the Spirit, and it names the Father as the one from whom the Spirit proceeds while distinguishing the Son's role as sending. The other texts establish mission, gift, possession, and relation, but none states that the Son is, with the Father, the eternal source of the Spirit's being. The same data can be organized under "from the Father through the Son" as well as "from the Father and the Son." Scripture also does not directly state the premise that temporal missions reproduce eternal relations of origin in the exact order required by the Filioque. The strongest case for retaining the confession therefore failed at the level of the exact proposition being confessed.

What Version 2.0 says instead. LST continues to confess one God in three coequal, coeternal persons, the full deity and personal distinction of the Spirit, and the Son's eternal generation. It states John 15:26 as written: the Spirit proceeds from the Father. It also confesses the Spirit's real relation to the Son and the Son's sending and pouring out of the Spirit. LST no longer confesses "from the Father and the Son" as a demonstrated biblical formula. Whether John 15:26 directly describes eternal origin rather than mission, and whether the Spirit's eternal relation to the Son should be expressed as "and the Son," "through the Son," or in another formulation, remains not demonstrated.

Why this triggers Version 2.0. The former Filioque sentence was not merely an argued possibility or a loose proof text. It was an affirmatively held confession-level proposition. Revision Resistance required LST to abandon that exact confession because Scripture did not demonstrate it. Under the major-version rule, surrendering an affirmatively held doctrine in substance advances the Systematic from the 1.x line to Version 2.0.

### ISSUE #6 - PARTIALLY SUSTAINED - ETERNAL CONSCIOUS PUNISHMENT

What stood in Version 1.11. Eternal conscious punishment was a defended synthesis said to rest on Revelation 14:11's no-rest clause, while the smoke clause was already acknowledged as insufficient by itself because of Isaiah 34.

**Case against revision.** Matthew 25:41 places condemned humans in the eternal fire prepared for the devil and his angels, and 25:46 sets eternal punishment beside eternal life. Revelation 14:9-11 directly depicts human conscious torment and no rest. Revelation 20:10 expressly assigns torment day and night forever and ever to the devil, beast, and false prophet, while 20:14-15 places the condemned in the same final lake of fire. Taken together, these texts provide a substantial canonical case for everlasting conscious punishment even though no single human-directed clause states the entire synthesis.

**What the challenge established.** The grammar of Revelation 14:11 does not attach "forever and ever" directly to the no-rest clause. The eternity phrase modifies the smoke going up, while the persons' unrest is described as day and night. Revelation 19:3 uses the same everlasting-smoke formula for judged Babylon after Revelation 18 depicts her destruction and disappearance. Therefore Revelation 14:11 alone cannot carry the everlasting duration of human conscious experience.

What Version 2.0 says instead. Eternal conscious punishment remains a defended canonical synthesis, but its basis is explicitly cumulative: Matthew 25:41, 46; Revelation 14:9-11; Revelation 20:10-15; and the wider judgment file. ## REVISION 18
The front matter, Chapter 1 method, Creed, Engagement Module, T.A.I.T. prompt, bootstrap, and reusable audit controls
The LST Governing Maxim
Effect: makes symmetrical confidence a front-and-center operating principle and rejects both theological overreach and manufactured middle-ground hesitation

What stood in Version 1.6. The system already taught the pieces of this rule. Scripture was final, theological confidence had to rise and fall with the evidence, inference had to be marked, unresolved questions had to remain visible, and Revision Resistance prevented objections from becoming revisions merely because they initially appeared strong. The system, however, stated these controls in several places rather than gathering them into one memorable rule. Its corrective emphasis could therefore be misread as though LST's aim were caution itself or a permanent middle position between traditions.

**Case against revision.** The strongest reason not to add a maxim was redundancy and bloat. Version 1.6 already contained the substance, and repeating methodological slogans can cause a document to grow without adding real control. A new maxim could also become an organizing axiom that competes with Scripture if it were treated as an independent source of doctrine.

Why the change was warranted. The proposed sentence does not add a new doctrine or a new authority. It compresses the existing method and corrects a real asymmetry in how theological humility can be heard. Scripture-governed theology must refuse unsupported certainty, but it must also refuse unsupported hesitation. Christian disagreement does not itself make the biblical text unclear. A system that always splits the difference can misrepresent Scripture just as surely as a system that overstates its inferences. The maxim therefore survives the resistance test precisely because it binds both directions and remains subordinate to Scripture.

What Version 1.7 says instead. The following is now the LST Governing Maxim:

"Living Systematic Theology seeks to state the theology of Scripture as nearly as fallible interpreters can, speaking with the certainty Scripture gives, refusing certainty Scripture does not give, adding nothing the text cannot sustain, and surrendering nothing the text requires us to confess."

It is immediately clarified: "Where Scripture speaks clearly, LST must speak clearly. Where Scripture does not settle a question, LST must not pretend that it has." The Systematic further states that the maxim is methodological rather than a second authority, that LST must not manufacture a middle position merely because Christians disagree, and that overstatement and habitual understatement are both errors when they misstate the strength of the biblical evidence.

Matching changes. Creed Version 1.5 places the maxim immediately after its authority statement. Engagement Module Version 1.3 applies the maxim to public theological speech and adds a final compliance check against both inflated certainty and unnecessary weakening. T.A.I.T. Prompt Version 1.4 applies the rule symmetrically to reasoning and confidence grading. The permanent bootstrap now identifies the Version 1.7 governing set and surfaces the maxim as high-priority current state. The reusable adversarial audit prompt now searches for underclaim as well as overclaim. LST_REBRANDING_LEDGER Version 1.2 updates the current governing filenames and records the maxim as part of the present architecture.

What was not changed. Scripture alone remains final authority. The current Systematic remains subordinate to Scripture. The Creed remains subordinate to the Systematic. The confidence categories remain confession, defended synthesis, argued possibility, and not demonstrated. No doctrine was moved merely to create a stronger voice, and no unresolved file was closed by rhetoric. The maxim governs how strongly LST speaks only after the evidence has been established.

## REVISION RESISTANCE TEST — IN-PLACE METHODOLOGICAL CLARIFICATION

October 5, 2026

Methodological clarification only. No LST doctrine changed. ENGAGEMENT_MODULE_LST_V1_2.txt and TAIT_PROMPT_LST_V1_2.txt retain their existing version numbers.

A mandatory CASE AGAINST REVISION control was added to the Engagement Module and mirrored in the T.A.I.T. prompt. Every proposed LST revision must now be treated first as a claim to test rather than a verdict to adopt. Before recommending that LST be qualified, reclassified, revised, abandoned, or advanced to a new numbered version, T.A.I.T. must deliberately build the strongest biblical case for retaining the current formulation, search for supporting texts the critic may have omitted, work those texts locally, and verify any external linguistic or historical claim carrying the defense.

The new control distinguishes a false doctrine from a doctrine whose existing argument is weak or incomplete. If Scripture sustains the current claim but LST used poor proof texts or failed to assemble its best evidence, the required correction is to strengthen the argument rather than change the doctrine. Only after the strongest case against revision has been tested may the strongest case for revision be weighed against it under the same exegetical standards.

The control also forbids assigning a revision number or saying a proposed change should become the next LST version before the resistance test is complete. It does not permit system protection: tradition, usefulness, architectural importance, or prior inclusion in LST cannot preserve a claim whose best biblical defense fails. The user remains the one who authorizes actual document revision.

Effect: adds a mandatory pre-revision defense stage so adversarial audits cannot become revisions merely because an objection initially appears strong.

## REVISION 13
Chapters 3 and 10 and the Conclusion
God's non-delight in the death of the wicked and the proposed qualifier "considered merely as ruin"
Effect: removes an unsupported qualifier from Ezekiel, distinguishes will from delight where Scripture does, and leaves the deeper tension open

What stood in Version 1.5. Chapters 3 and 10 said that God does not delight in destruction or in the death of the wicked "considered merely as ruin." Chapter 10 placed that qualifier inside what God "declares" in Ezekiel 18 and 33.

Why it had to change. Ezekiel 18:23, 32 and 33:11 say that God has no pleasure in the death of the wicked but in the wicked turning and living. The qualifier is not Ezekiel's. The resistance test then showed that the counter-file also had to be worked more carefully. First Samuel 2:25 and Isaiah 53:10 use the Hebrew construction for God's being willing or minded to bring about a death, not the construction Ezekiel uses for delighting in death; Judges 13:23 supplies a close grammatical parallel. Lamentations 3:32-33, 37-38 says that God causes grief while not afflicting from His heart. Deuteronomy 28:63 remains the sharp pressure because it says the LORD would rejoice over covenant breakers to destroy them, as He had rejoiced to do them good.

What Version 1.6 says instead. Ezekiel's own contrast is stated without an inserted qualifier. LST distinguishes God's willing a judicial death from delighting in that death where the biblical constructions warrant the distinction. It also names Deuteronomy 28:63 rather than explaining it away. The account that God does not delight in death considered in itself but delights in the justice of judgment is retained only as an argued possibility in LST's own voice, not as Ezekiel's wording or as a demonstrated canonical solution. The Conclusion adds the Ezekiel 33:11 / Deuteronomy 28:63 relation to the visible pressure file.

What was not changed. God's call to repentance remains sincere. His decree remains comprehensive and undefeated. Scripture's descriptions of His grief, lament, judgment, and purpose remain truthful. No single philosophical formula is permitted to silence either register.


## REVISION 14
Chapters 13, 15, 17, and 23
The future territorial realization and the retrospective correction of Revision 3
Effect: partially reverses Revision 3 by restoring a narrower territorial proposal to argued possibility while leaving a mapped Abrahamic-boundary polity not demonstrated

What stood after Revision 3. Version 1.1 reclassified a distinct penultimate administration in which ethnic Israel holds the Abrahamic boundaries as a territorial polity from argued possibility to not demonstrated. The stated reason included the claim that no positive text describing an intermediate public territorial order had been produced.

Why that rationale could not stand unchanged. Zechariah 14:3-21 supplies positive evidence for a narrower proposition. The LORD comes with His holy ones, becomes King over all the earth, and afterward surviving nations continue to go up to Jerusalem to worship under conditions that still include refusal, drought, and plague. Those features are not identical on their face with Revelation 21-22's deathless final state. Isaiah 65:17-25 and Ezekiel 37:21-28 add supporting pressure. The resistance test therefore found that Revision 3 had wrongly treated the absence of proof for the original mapped polity as though no narrower post-coming territorial proposal had positive biblical support.

The resistance test also limited the restoration. Ezekiel 47-48 is not counted as independent literal territorial proof while Chapter 18 reads the sanctuary-and-land complex as visionary-typological. Deuteronomy 30 names return to the fathers' land but does not by itself locate a further penultimate stage. Zechariah 14 also contains Feast-of-Booths and sanctuary language, which must be treated with the same literal and canonical controls as its land language. Revelation 21-22 takes up major restoration imagery, and 1 Corinthians 15:54 and 2 Thessalonians 1:7-10 press against a post-coming order in which death and rebellion continue.

What Version 1.6 says instead. A mapped penultimate polity possessing the Abrahamic boundaries remains not demonstrated. A narrower proposition is restored to argued possibility: after Christ's appearing and before the final state, restored Israel and Jerusalem may stand within a historical order to which surviving nations come to worship the King. Zechariah 14 carries the proposal most directly. Neither this reading nor the final-state reading of the restoration oracles is treated as demonstrated.


## REVISION 15
Chapter 17, Prophecy, the Millennium, and the Gathering of the Saints
The ranking of the consecutive and recapitulation readings of Revelation 20 and the retrospective correction of Revision 5
Effect: retains the withdrawal of "the most natural movement" but replaces the faulty rationale and completes the rival-reading file

What stood after Revision 5. Version 1.2 withdrew the phrase "the most natural movement of the visions as they stand" because the chapter also admitted that neither reading was excluded and that both paid interpretive costs.

Why that rationale had to change. A viable rival reading does not by itself prevent one reading from being stronger. The resistance test found that the local grammar of Revelation 20:4-6 strongly favors the consecutive reading: the same verb in the same form governs both groups, "the rest of the dead" marks the first group as having been among the dead, until makes the second group's coming to life the delayed counterpart of the first, and John calls the first event a resurrection. Revelation 2:8 and 13:14 use the same verb for coming back to life after death.

The same test also found that the recapitulation reading had not been given its strongest structural and canonical evidence. Revelation 16:14 and 20:8 use the same gathering-for-battle language; Revelation 19:17-21 and 20:8-9 both draw on Ezekiel's Gog complex; Revelation 20:2 repeats the dragon's titles from 12:9; and John 6:40, John 12:48, Acts 24:15, and 1 Corinthians 15:54 place real pressure toward a single end-time resurrection-and-judgment complex. Revelation 20:8 also permits the purpose-limited reading of Satan's deception to be drawn from the same paragraph rather than imported wholly from outside it.

What Version 1.6 says instead. On the grammar of Revelation 20:4-6, the consecutive reading has the stronger case. On the structure of Revelation 12-20 and on the single-end texts, recapitulation has substantial counterevidence. LST continues to hold the future millennial interval as a defended synthesis, but it does not presently claim that the consecutive reading has the stronger case overall and it does not restore the phrase "most natural movement." The earlier withdrawal stands on a corrected rationale.

## REVISION 17
Chapter 10, God's Revealed Will and the Universal Gospel Call
The two-wills distinction and the retrospective correction of Revision 8
Effect: reverses Revision 8's governing rationale and restores the distinction as a guard against reduction without claiming that it explains the metaphysical fit

What stood after Revision 8. Version 1.2 withdrew the claim that the preceptive/decretive distinction "prevents two reductions" and stated that a distinction which cannot be shown to cohere has not prevented anything.

Why that rationale had to change. The resistance test showed that the sentence confused two different tasks. A distinction may prevent one biblical relation from being collapsed into another without explaining exhaustively how the two relations meet in God. Scripture itself gives the relevant distinctions: one event can carry God's good intention and human evil intention (Genesis 50:20; Acts 2:23), the secret and revealed things are distinguished in relation to obedience (Deuteronomy 29:29), and God causes grief while not afflicting from His heart (Lamentations 3:32-33, 37-38). LST already uses analogous distinctions elsewhere, including confessing Christ's two natures without claiming to explain the mystery of their union.

What Version 1.6 says instead. The preceptive/decretive distinction guards against two reductions. God's revealed saving address must not be reduced to theater, and His decretive purpose must not be reduced to a purpose that can finally fail. The distinction names biblical relations; it does not exhaustively explain how they cohere in the one simple God. Christ's lament and other revealed desires remain real even where creaturely unwillingness resists them; that does not imply that the decretive purpose fails.

## REVISION 12
Chapters 9, 11, 19, 23, and the Conclusion
The relation between present justification and final judgment according to works
Effect: qualifies the role of works, strengthens the defended synthesis, and opens Romans 2:13 as a visible exegetical pressure

What stood in Version 1.4. Chapter 9 called good works necessary fruit and evidence of living faith and said they were never a second ground of acceptance. Chapter 19 said good works were considered at final judgment as evidence of a person's deeds and relation to Christ, never as a second means of earning justification. Chapter 23 said judgment according to works tells the truth about deeds and relation to Christ and that the public day does not reopen justification as a second path. Those claims were stated without a confidence grade and without working Romans 2:13 or the full James 2 argument.

Why it had to change. Romans 2:6 says God renders to each person according to works. Romans 2:13 says the doers of the law will be justified, using future justification language inside the judgment scene. Second Corinthians 5:10 speaks of receiving what is due for deeds done in the body. Those sentences cannot be fully represented by calling works evidence. At the same time, Romans 4:4-5 excludes works as a debt-producing ground of the ungodly person's justification, Romans 5:9 joins present justification by Christ's blood to future salvation from wrath, Romans 8:1 and 8:33-34 place the believer's final security in God's justifying verdict and Christ's finished and interceding work, and John 5:24 says the believer has passed from death to life and does not come into judgment. The file therefore supports a stronger denial of a second meritorious ground while requiring more modesty about exactly how the future judgment and Romans 2:13 relate to present justification.

James 2 also required fuller work. James says a person is justified by works and not by faith alone. LST continues to read this as the vindication and manifestation of living faith because the paragraph begins with a workless claim to faith that cannot save, speaks of showing faith by works, contrasts demonic belief, and says Abraham's faith worked with and was completed by his works. That reading remains LST's contextual conclusion. It is not treated as a lexical rule that justify simply means show righteous.

The Romans 2 fork is left visible. One reading hears 2:6-16 as the law's true standard within Paul's movement toward the conclusion that no flesh will be justified by works of the law in 3:20. Another reading hears real Spirit-made doers in the scene, with 2:28-29 and 8:4 placing pressure in that direction. Chapter 13's existing treatment of inward circumcision as the Spirit's work means LST cannot dismiss that pressure. Neither reading is declared demonstrated in Version 1.5.

Matthew 18:23-35 is added to Chapter 11's warning file. The parable depicts a forgiven debt later restored after merciless conduct and applies the warning to the disciples. It speaks in the language of forgiveness rather than Paul's technical argument about forensic justification, so it does not by itself establish lost justification. It nevertheless must be explained by any perseverance synthesis and may not be neutralized because it is difficult.

What Version 1.5 says instead. Good works are necessary fruit of living faith and genuinely show that faith, but evidence is not treated as the exhaustive category for their role. Scripture says final judgment is according to works and that deeds are genuinely considered. Works are not the meritorious ground of acceptance before God. LST holds as a defended synthesis that the future verdict is consistent with, confirms, and does not replace the justification already received through faith in Christ. Exactly how Romans 2:13's future justification language relates to the present verdict remains not demonstrated and is now listed among the system's visible pressures.

Matching changes. Creed Version 1.3 now summarizes the Version 1.5 formulation and adds Romans 2:13 to its unresolved file. T.A.I.T. Prompt Version 1.2 now points to Systematic Version 1.5 and Creed Version 1.3 and contains a control preventing future replies from either reducing works to evidence only or making them the meritorious ground of final acceptance. Engagement Module Version 1.2 required no doctrinal rewrite: it already requires the current Systematic to govern truth, requires explicit statements to be distinguished from inference, and forbids protecting LST from difficult texts.

What was not changed. Justification remains God's forensic verdict received through faith apart from works. Christ's obedience and blood remain the meritorious ground. Union with Christ, adoption, sanctification, the necessity of good works, perseverance, bodily resurrection, and final judgment remain. The revision concerns how LST describes the role of works at the final judgment and the confidence with which it relates Romans 2:13 to present justification.

## REVISION 11
Chapter 9, Salvation by Sovereign Grace
"Regeneration is logically prior to saving faith"
Effect: lowers confidence and reclassifies the exact ordering as not demonstrated

What stood in Version 1.3. Chapter 9 said without qualification: "Regeneration is logically prior to saving faith: the Spirit gives the new heart from which the sinner truly believes." The chapter cited John 3:3-8, John 6:44-45, Romans 10:14-17, and 1 Peter 1:23-25 under a blended sentence about effectual calling, life, repentance, and faith.

Why it had to change. The examination found that the passages establish several things firmly but do not establish the exact relation the sentence named. John 6 places the Father's giving, drawing, teaching, life-giving action, and granting before the coming that stands parallel to believing. Acts 16:14 places the Lord's opening of Lydia's heart before her heeding the preached word. Those texts establish prior and effective divine action. They do not identify every prior act as regeneration. John 3 requires birth from above and from the Spirit but does not state its logical order against believing. James 1:18 and 1 Peter 1:23-25 establish God's agency and the Word as the instrument of the birth without ordering birth and faith. Romans 10:14-17 establishes the Word as the instrument of faith without naming regeneration. First John 5:1 identifies the believer as one who has been born of God, but its perfect construction, especially alongside John's similar descriptions of righteousness and love, does not by itself encode causal order. Galatians 3:2 and Ephesians 1:13 likewise may not be turned into regeneration texts without first demonstrating that identification.

The decisive symmetry point was this: John 6 and Acts 16 cannot be used both to deny that the prior divine act is demonstrably regeneration and then to prove regeneration's position before faith. An undemonstrated identification cannot carry an ordering conclusion. The evidence establishes that saving faith depends upon prior sovereign divine action and that regeneration is God's life-giving work. It does not presently distinguish the exact logical place of regeneration relative to faith.

What Version 1.4 says instead. Saving faith is not autonomous. No one comes unless the Father draws and grants it, and God acts effectively through the proclaimed Word. The new birth is God's act, not human self-generation, and God brings His people forth through His Word. LST therefore confesses prior sovereign divine action and God's monergistic life-giving work while classifying the exact logical ordering of regeneration and saving faith as not demonstrated. Drawing, granting, teaching, heart-opening, Spirit-given life, receiving or sealing by the Spirit, effectual calling, and new birth are not collapsed into one technical moment without positive textual grounds. LST still denies a prolonged condition of consciously regenerate unbelief, and the person whom God saves truly and willingly repents and believes.

Matching changes. The Conclusion now names the regeneration-faith order among the visible unresolved pressures. Creed Version 1.2 removes the flat statement that regeneration precedes saving faith and records the exact order as not demonstrated. The T.A.I.T. prompt is aligned so that future answers may not teach an exact ordo above the evidence.

What was not changed. Salvation remains by sovereign grace. Human inability, the necessity and efficacy of divine initiative, the gift-character of faith and repentance, the sincerity of the external call, union with Christ, justification by faith apart from works, adoption, sanctification, perseverance, and glorification were not reopened by this revision.

## REVISION 9
Chapter 23, Death, Judgment, Resurrection, and the New Creation
The unqualified use of Isaiah 65:17-25 as a direct description of the deathless final state
Effect: qualifies and opens an exegetical file

What stood in Version 1.2. Chapter 23 grouped Isaiah 65:17-25 with Romans 8:19-23, 2 Peter 3:10-13, and Revelation 21-22 as naming the new heavens and new earth, while the Conclusion separately listed Isaiah 65 among the pressures not to be declared solved.

Why it had to change. Isaiah 65:20 negates infant death and an old man failing to fill his days, then states positively that the youth dies at a hundred years. The Hebrew verb is the ordinary verb for dying. Supplying a verb of estimation in order to make the death disappear repeats the kind of move LST had already refused in Ezekiel 40-48 and Revelation 14:11. Verse 23 also speaks of offspring and descendants, while Revelation 21:4 expressly says death shall be no more. ## REVISION 1
Chapter 8, The Holy Spirit and His Gifts
Bounded cessationism

## REVISION 2
Chapter 17, Prophecy, the Millennium, and the Gathering of the Saints
The content of the millennial interval
Effect: qualifies and lowers confidence

What stood in Version 1.0. A future millennial reign before the last judgment, held as a defended interpretation of Revelation 20 in canonical context, described as the open exercise of the rule Christ already possesses.

Why it had to change. That description restates Christ's present session. The recapitulation reading is written out at its real strength, where Version 1.0 had only said it must be tested, and its two costs are named: the narrowing of the purpose clause against Revelation 12:9, and the double sense given to one verb inside the single contrast John draws between the souls who lived and the rest of the dead who did not. Chapter 15, The Land Promise and the Inheritance

## REVISION 4
Chapter 13, Promise, Fulfillment, and Historical Actualization
The closure-and-enlargement distinction
Effect: strengthens the method by making an unstated premise explicit

What stood in Version 1.0. The bilateral continuation rule of Chapter 2, that unchanged continuation and cancellation each need positive grounds and that the absence of a cancellation formula proves neither.

Why it had to change. The rule was stated without the distinction that keeps it bilateral, and LST was using that distinction in practice without writing it. A text that closes a function settles whether the thing still happens; a text that enlarges a scope does not by itself settle the stages by which the larger reality is administered. Hebrews 10:18 is a closure, which is why Chapter 18 refuses a guilt-removing cultus. Romans 4:13 and Hebrews 11:8-16 are enlargements, which is why they do not settle the number of administrations. An unstated premise doing load-bearing work is exactly what the system's own eisegesis controls forbid.

What Version 1.1 says instead. The distinction is written into Chapter 13 and expressly binds LST in both directions. It is stated there that an enlargement neither cancels a promise nor licenses a reserve, that where only an enlargement is in hand the stages are undetermined rather than defaulting to an additional stage, and that treating the absence of a closure text as positive ground for continuation is the argument from silence Chapter 2 forbids.
