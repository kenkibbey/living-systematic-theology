LIVING SYSTEMATIC THEOLOGY
REVISION RECORD

A companion document to Living Systematic Theology. It is not a theology and carries no doctrinal authority. It records what changed between versions, why, and what the change cost.

Scripture does not change when LST changes. This file records corrections to a fallible system, not development in the Word of God.

Each entry names the chapter, the earlier formulation, the reason the change was required, and whether the revision strengthens, qualifies, lowers confidence in, or abandons what stood before. Where a change came out of a public examination, that is said plainly rather than presented as internal refinement.

VERSION 1.9
October 6, 2026
Supersedes Version 1.8
=================================================

One material exegetical and doctrinal-clarification revision, arising from a Church of Christ challenge to perseverance, continuance, and good works. The challenge did not overturn LST's defended synthesis that all who are truly savingly united to Christ will finally persevere and be glorified. It did expose places where LST could speak as though the necessary conditions of continuance were less than real and where the warning-text file was incomplete.

REVISION 20
Chapters 9, 11, 19, and 23
Perseverance, necessary continuance, good works, and the forfeiture challenge
Effect: retains the perseverance synthesis, strengthens the necessity of continuing faith and Spirit-enabled obedience, expands the warning-text file, and prevents the synthesis from being built into the vocabulary of individual warnings

What stood in Version 1.8. Chapter 11 said preservation is God's act and perseverance is the believer's Spirit-given continuance in faith, repentance, and obedience. It also said that these biblical conditions were not ornaments, but described endurance as "not a second condition standing beside God's keeping" because the continuing is itself promised. The chapter already treated Hebrews 6, Hebrews 10:29, John 15, Romans 11, Luke 8:13, 1 John 2:24-25, and Matthew 18:23-35 as genuine pressure and explicitly called the final-apostasy conclusion a canonical synthesis rather than a definition imposed on individual words. Chapters 9, 19, and 23 already denied that evidence exhausts the biblical role of works and already taught judgment according to works without making works the meritorious ground of justification.

Case against revision. The strongest case for retaining the perseverance doctrine remains substantial. Jesus says He will lose none of those given to Him but raise them at the last day, and that His sheep receive eternal life and will never perish (John 6:37-40; 10:27-29). Romans 8:28-39 carries the called and justified to glorification and excludes created powers from separating believers from God's love in Christ. Believers are guarded by God's power through faith for the salvation ready to be revealed (1 Peter 1:3-5). Paul says Christ will sustain His people to the end because God is faithful (1 Corinthians 1:8-9), expresses confidence that God will complete the work He began (Philippians 1:6), and joins preservation to God's faithfulness at Christ's coming (1 Thessalonians 5:23-24). Jude praises God as able to keep His people from stumbling and present them blameless. The challenge's warning texts are serious, but none explicitly states that a person whom the text identifies as regenerate and justified later becomes unregenerate or unjustified.

Why no doctrinal reversal was warranted. Hebrews 10:29 naturally refers "sanctified" to the offender and therefore remains the sharpest local pressure, but sanctification language alone does not state the full systematic proposition that regeneration and forensic justification were possessed and later reversed. John 15 says the unfruitful branch is "in me," Galatians 5:4 says "severed from Christ" and "fallen from grace," Romans 11 warns one who stands by faith that he can be cut off, and 2 Peter 2:20-22 describes real escape through the knowledge of Christ followed by renewed entanglement. Those expressions may not be thinned. At the same time, none of those passages independently states the reversal of new birth or justification. James 2 tests a claimed, workless faith that cannot save; it does not first establish that the person possessed saving union and then lost it. The warning file therefore places real pressure on perseverance without presently overturning the positive preservation file.

What the challenge successfully established. A promised condition is still a real condition. LST should not speak as though God's promise to preserve His people makes perseverance less necessary. Hebrews 3:14, Colossians 1:22-23, Matthew 24:13, Romans 8:13, Hebrews 12:14, Revelation 2:10, and Romans 11:22 all give continuance real conditional force. Persevering faith, repentance, holiness, and Spirit-enabled obedience are genuinely necessary to final salvation. They do not purchase justification and are not an independent meritorious ground. In LST's present synthesis, God preserves His people precisely by bringing them through these necessary conditions and means.

What Version 1.9 says instead. The core doctrine remains a defended synthesis: all who are truly savingly united to Christ will finally persevere and be glorified, and final apostasy manifests absence of saving union rather than destruction of new birth. The wording now makes explicit that this is a canonical synthesis from the whole preservation-and-warning file, not the lexical meaning of sanctified, in me, fallen from grace, escaped, stand by faith, or any other warning expression. Galatians 5:4 and 2 Peter 2:20-22 are added to the explicit pressure file. Romans 8:13, Hebrews 12:14, and Revelation 2:10 are added to the continuance file. Chapters 9, 19, and 23 now say more plainly that Spirit-enabled holiness and obedience are necessary in the ordained path to final salvation while remaining non-meritorious.

Matching changes. Creed Version 1.7 now summarizes the necessity of persevering faith, repentance, and Spirit-enabled obedience while retaining final perseverance as a defended synthesis and naming the expanded warning file. T.A.I.T. Prompt Version 1.6 now prevents future answers from either treating continuance as merely optional evidence or turning warning vocabulary into lost regeneration by definition. The bootstrap now identifies the Version 1.9 governing set and surfaces Revision 20 as high-priority current state. Engagement Module Version 1.3 required no revision because its existing Governing Maxim and Revision Resistance controls already govern the change.

What was not changed. LST did not adopt the doctrine that a truly regenerate or justified person can finally forfeit salvation. It did not make good works the meritorious ground of present or final acceptance. It did not weaken the warning passages into hypothetical theater. It did not make perseverance optional. The preserved synthesis must continue to answer the expanded warning file, and if a warning passage is later shown to establish the reversal of saving union itself, LST must change.

VERSION 1.8
October 6, 2026
Supersedes Version 1.7
=================================================

One material doctrinal and exegetical revision, arising from a direct challenge to Chapter 20 from a Church of Christ reading of the baptism texts. The challenge partially prevailed. LST retains believer's baptism and immersion as its ordinary practice, but withdraws categorical language that reduced baptism to a sign of saving benefits already completed before the rite and that treated faith alone as the stated creator of union in the baptism passages.

REVISION 19
Chapter 20, The Church, Its Mission, and Its Ordinances
Baptism: forgiveness, washing, union-with-Christ language, salvation, timing, and mode
Effect: strengthens the positive force of the baptism texts, withdraws unsupported negations, opens the exact causal and temporal relation as unresolved, and retains immersion as ordinary without making it the demonstrated essence of validity

What stood in Version 1.7. Chapter 20 said that baptism's saving and washing texts bound the ordinance to calling on Christ and His resurrection, but then characterized the rite primarily as confession and sign of a work already done. It said, "Water does not create union with Christ. Faith in the risen Christ does," and later, "Baptism does not regenerate. It does not justify. It does not confer union by the act itself." It affirmed believer's baptism ordinarily by immersion while saying the burial symbolism did not independently command the quantity of water and that mode was not the essence of the ordinance. The chapter worked Romans 6, 1 Peter 3:21, Acts 22:16, and Colossians 2:11-12, but it did not work Acts 2:38, Galatians 3:26-27, or Colossians 2:13 in the baptism file.

Case against revision. The strongest case for retaining the prior formulation comes from passages that refuse a universal rule making water baptism the exact moment of every saving benefit. Cornelius hears the gospel, receives the Holy Spirit before water baptism, and is afterward commanded to be baptized (Acts 10:43-48). Peter later interprets the Gentile event by saying God gave them the Spirit and, in Acts 15:7-9, that God cleansed their hearts by faith. Ephesians 1:13 joins hearing, believing, and sealing with the Spirit without naming water baptism as the temporal hinge. First Corinthians 1:14-17 distinguishes Paul's commission to preach the gospel from the act of baptizing, which makes it difficult to identify the physical rite as a second meritorious ground or as the indispensable efficient cause of justification. Romans 3-4 continues to locate the meritorious ground wholly outside human works, in Christ received through faith. These texts prevent the Church of Christ proposal from being adopted at full strength.

Why the former wording nevertheless could not stand. Acts 2:38 directly joins repentance and baptism in Jesus Christ's name with forgiveness of sins and the gift of the Holy Spirit. Acts 22:16 joins baptism, washing away sins, and calling on the Lord's name. Romans 6:3-4 speaks of baptism into Christ and His death and burial with Him through baptism. Galatians 3:26-27 places sonship through faith beside the explanation that those baptized into Christ have put on Christ. Colossians 2:12-13 joins burial in baptism, raising through faith in God's powerful working, being made alive with Christ, and forgiveness of trespasses. First Peter 3:21 says baptism now saves, while denying that Peter means mere removal of dirt and locating the saving relation in an appeal to God through Christ's resurrection. The former paragraph said it did not deny these sentences but then supplied a systematic reduction that made baptism chiefly a sign of benefits already completed elsewhere. It also omitted several of the passages most directly joining baptism to forgiveness and union. Under LST's Governing Maxim, that was an underclaim.

What Version 1.8 says instead. Baptism is Christ's commanded initiatory ordinance for professing believers and belongs integrally to the apostolic conversion pattern. LST now states plainly that Scripture directly joins baptism to forgiveness, washing, union-with-Christ language, burial and rising with Christ, and salvation. Baptism therefore may not be reduced to an empty symbol or to a picture detached from those saving realities. At the same time, the canon does not demonstrate that physical water is a magical or meritorious cause or that baptism is universally the exact temporal instant at which regeneration, justification, forgiveness, or saving union first occurs. Cornelius remains the decisive canonical pressure against such a universal timing claim. The exact causal and temporal relation among faith, repentance, baptism, the Spirit's life-giving work, forgiveness, justification, and saving union is now classified as not demonstrated. Christ's blood remains the sole meritorious ground; the Spirit gives life; faith receives Christ; baptism is commanded and soteriologically weighty rather than optional theater.

Mode. Immersion remains LST's ordinary practice because the burial-and-rising language of Romans 6 and Colossians 2 and the movement into and out of water in Acts 8 strongly fit it. The challenge did not establish that Scripture expressly makes immersion so constitutive of baptism that every other application of water is invalid. LST therefore retains immersion positively without turning the positive case into an exclusion above the evidence.

Matching changes. Creed Version 1.6 now summarizes baptism as integrally joined to forgiveness, washing, union-with-Christ language, and salvation while listing the exact causal and temporal relation among baptism and the saving benefits as unresolved. T.A.I.T. Prompt Version 1.5 carries a binding baptism control so future answers neither collapse baptism into a bare sign nor turn it into a magical or universal timing mechanism. The bootstrap now identifies the Version 1.8 governing set and surfaces Revision 19 as high-priority current state. Engagement Module Version 1.3 required no revision because its existing Governing Maxim and Revision Resistance controls already require both overclaim and underclaim to be corrected.

What was not changed. LST still affirms believer's baptism rather than infant baptism as its present defended synthesis. Household texts still do not inventory infants, and Colossians 2 still does not state a one-for-one infant-circumcision to infant-baptism replacement rule. Christ's blood remains the sole meritorious ground of forgiveness and justification. The Spirit remains the giver of life. Faith and repentance remain genuine and necessary responses. Baptism is not treated as a human payment that earns salvation.

VERSION 1.7
October 6, 2026
Supersedes Version 1.6
=================================================

One material methodological and architectural change. No substantive doctrinal conclusion was reversed. Version 1.7 places a single governing maxim at the front of LST so the system is protected from two opposite failures: claiming more certainty than Scripture gives and withholding certainty Scripture actually gives.

REVISION 18
The front matter, Chapter 1 method, Creed, Engagement Module, T.A.I.T. prompt, bootstrap, and reusable audit controls
The LST Governing Maxim
Effect: makes symmetrical confidence a front-and-center operating principle and rejects both theological overreach and manufactured middle-ground hesitation

What stood in Version 1.6. The system already taught the pieces of this rule. Scripture was final, theological confidence had to rise and fall with the evidence, inference had to be marked, unresolved questions had to remain visible, and Revision Resistance prevented objections from becoming revisions merely because they initially appeared strong. The system, however, stated these controls in several places rather than gathering them into one memorable rule. Its corrective emphasis could therefore be misread as though LST's aim were caution itself or a permanent middle position between traditions.

Case against revision. The strongest reason not to add a maxim was redundancy and bloat. Version 1.6 already contained the substance, and repeating methodological slogans can cause a document to grow without adding real control. A new maxim could also become an organizing axiom that competes with Scripture if it were treated as an independent source of doctrine.

Why the change was warranted. The proposed sentence does not add a new doctrine or a new authority. It compresses the existing method and corrects a real asymmetry in how theological humility can be heard. Scripture-governed theology must refuse unsupported certainty, but it must also refuse unsupported hesitation. Christian disagreement does not itself make the biblical text unclear. A system that always splits the difference can misrepresent Scripture just as surely as a system that overstates its inferences. The maxim therefore survives the resistance test precisely because it binds both directions and remains subordinate to Scripture.

What Version 1.7 says instead. The following is now the LST Governing Maxim:

"Living Systematic Theology seeks to state the theology of Scripture as nearly as fallible interpreters can, speaking with the certainty Scripture gives, refusing certainty Scripture does not give, adding nothing the text cannot sustain, and surrendering nothing the text requires us to confess."

It is immediately clarified: "Where Scripture speaks clearly, LST must speak clearly. Where Scripture does not settle a question, LST must not pretend that it has." The Systematic further states that the maxim is methodological rather than a second authority, that LST must not manufacture a middle position merely because Christians disagree, and that overstatement and habitual understatement are both errors when they misstate the strength of the biblical evidence.

Matching changes. Creed Version 1.5 places the maxim immediately after its authority statement. Engagement Module Version 1.3 applies the maxim to public theological speech and adds a final compliance check against both inflated certainty and unnecessary weakening. T.A.I.T. Prompt Version 1.4 applies the rule symmetrically to reasoning and confidence grading. The permanent bootstrap now identifies the Version 1.7 governing set and surfaces the maxim as high-priority current state. The reusable adversarial audit prompt now searches for underclaim as well as overclaim. LST_REBRANDING_LEDGER Version 1.2 updates the current governing filenames and records the maxim as part of the present architecture.

What was not changed. Scripture alone remains final authority. The current Systematic remains subordinate to Scripture. The Creed remains subordinate to the Systematic. The confidence categories remain confession, defended synthesis, argued possibility, and not demonstrated. No doctrine was moved merely to create a stronger voice, and no unresolved file was closed by rhetoric. The maxim governs how strongly LST speaks only after the evidence has been established.

A new subsection, "WHEN SYSTEM PROTECTION BECOMES EXCLUSION," was added under the existing System Protection controls. It addresses the point at which an exchange visibly shifts from answering a theological argument to attempting to ban, remove, silence, or exclude the person raising it. Effect: strengthens engagement method and output reliability without changing LST doctrine.

REVISION RESISTANCE TEST - IN-PLACE METHODOLOGICAL CLARIFICATION
October 5, 2026
=================================================

Methodological clarification only. No LST doctrine changed. ENGAGEMENT_MODULE_LST_V1_2.txt and TAIT_PROMPT_LST_V1_2.txt retain their existing version numbers.

A mandatory CASE AGAINST REVISION control was added to the Engagement Module and mirrored in the T.A.I.T. prompt. Every proposed LST revision must now be treated first as a claim to test rather than a verdict to adopt. Before recommending that LST be qualified, reclassified, revised, abandoned, or advanced to a new numbered version, T.A.I.T. must deliberately build the strongest biblical case for retaining the current formulation, search for supporting texts the critic may have omitted, work those texts locally, and verify any external linguistic or historical claim carrying the defense.

The new control distinguishes a false doctrine from a doctrine whose existing argument is weak or incomplete. If Scripture sustains the current claim but LST used poor proof texts or failed to assemble its best evidence, the required correction is to strengthen the argument rather than change the doctrine. Only after the strongest case against revision has been tested may the strongest case for revision be weighed against it under the same exegetical standards.

The control also forbids assigning a revision number or saying a proposed change should become the next LST version before the resistance test is complete. It does not permit system protection: tradition, usefulness, architectural importance, or prior inclusion in LST cannot preserve a claim whose best biblical defense fails. The user remains the one who authorizes actual document revision.

Effect: adds a mandatory pre-revision defense stage so adversarial audits cannot become revisions merely because an objection initially appears strong.

VERSION 1.6
October 6, 2026
Supersedes Version 1.5
=================================================

Five material changes, arising from the first retrospective application of the Revision Resistance Test to LST's existing revision history and to the unadopted proposal concerning God's pleasure in judgment. Revisions 1 through 12 were treated as claims to test rather than as protected corrections. Revisions 1, 4, 7, 10, 11, and 12 survived the strongest case for their earlier formulations. Revisions 2 and 9 retained their core corrections. Revisions 3, 5, 6, and 8 required further correction after their own rationales were tested symmetrically. The earlier proposed change to "considered merely as ruin" also required qualification before adoption.

The governing result is not a return to Version 1.0 or Version 1.1. Version 1.6 keeps what the earlier revisions established where the resistance test sustained it, restores only what positive biblical evidence warrants, and records where an earlier revision itself had spoken too strongly.


REVISION 13
Chapters 3 and 10 and the Conclusion
God's non-delight in the death of the wicked and the proposed qualifier "considered merely as ruin"
Effect: removes an unsupported qualifier from Ezekiel, distinguishes will from delight where Scripture does, and leaves the deeper tension open

What stood in Version 1.5. Chapters 3 and 10 said that God does not delight in destruction or in the death of the wicked "considered merely as ruin." Chapter 10 placed that qualifier inside what God "declares" in Ezekiel 18 and 33.

Why it had to change. Ezekiel 18:23, 32 and 33:11 say that God has no pleasure in the death of the wicked but in the wicked turning and living. The qualifier is not Ezekiel's. The resistance test then showed that the counter-file also had to be worked more carefully. First Samuel 2:25 and Isaiah 53:10 use the Hebrew construction for God's being willing or minded to bring about a death, not the construction Ezekiel uses for delighting in death; Judges 13:23 supplies a close grammatical parallel. Lamentations 3:32-33, 37-38 says that God causes grief while not afflicting from His heart. Deuteronomy 28:63 remains the sharp pressure because it says the LORD would rejoice over covenant breakers to destroy them, as He had rejoiced to do them good.

What Version 1.6 says instead. Ezekiel's own contrast is stated without an inserted qualifier. LST distinguishes God's willing a judicial death from delighting in that death where the biblical constructions warrant the distinction. It also names Deuteronomy 28:63 rather than explaining it away. The account that God does not delight in death considered in itself but delights in the justice of judgment is retained only as an argued possibility in LST's own voice, not as Ezekiel's wording or as a demonstrated canonical solution. The Conclusion adds the Ezekiel 33:11 / Deuteronomy 28:63 relation to the visible pressure file.

What was not changed. God's call to repentance remains sincere. His decree remains comprehensive and undefeated. Scripture's descriptions of His grief, lament, judgment, and purpose remain truthful. No single philosophical formula is permitted to silence either register.


REVISION 14
Chapters 13, 15, 17, and 23
The future territorial realization and the retrospective correction of Revision 3
Effect: partially reverses Revision 3 by restoring a narrower territorial proposal to argued possibility while leaving a mapped Abrahamic-boundary polity not demonstrated

What stood after Revision 3. Version 1.1 reclassified a distinct penultimate administration in which ethnic Israel holds the Abrahamic boundaries as a territorial polity from argued possibility to not demonstrated. The stated reason included the claim that no positive text describing an intermediate public territorial order had been produced.

Why that rationale could not stand unchanged. Zechariah 14:3-21 supplies positive evidence for a narrower proposition. The LORD comes with His holy ones, becomes King over all the earth, and afterward surviving nations continue to go up to Jerusalem to worship under conditions that still include refusal, drought, and plague. Those features are not identical on their face with Revelation 21-22's deathless final state. Isaiah 65:17-25 and Ezekiel 37:21-28 add supporting pressure. The resistance test therefore found that Revision 3 had wrongly treated the absence of proof for the original mapped polity as though no narrower post-coming territorial proposal had positive biblical support.

The resistance test also limited the restoration. Ezekiel 47-48 is not counted as independent literal territorial proof while Chapter 18 reads the sanctuary-and-land complex as visionary-typological. Deuteronomy 30 names return to the fathers' land but does not by itself locate a further penultimate stage. Zechariah 14 also contains Feast-of-Booths and sanctuary language, which must be treated with the same literal and canonical controls as its land language. Revelation 21-22 takes up major restoration imagery, and 1 Corinthians 15:54 and 2 Thessalonians 1:7-10 press against a post-coming order in which death and rebellion continue.

What Version 1.6 says instead. A mapped penultimate polity possessing the Abrahamic boundaries remains not demonstrated. A narrower proposition is restored to argued possibility: after Christ's appearing and before the final state, restored Israel and Jerusalem may stand within a historical order to which surviving nations come to worship the King. Zechariah 14 carries the proposal most directly. Neither this reading nor the final-state reading of the restoration oracles is treated as demonstrated.


REVISION 15
Chapter 17, Prophecy, the Millennium, and the Gathering of the Saints
The ranking of the consecutive and recapitulation readings of Revelation 20 and the retrospective correction of Revision 5
Effect: retains the withdrawal of "the most natural movement" but replaces the faulty rationale and completes the rival-reading file

What stood after Revision 5. Version 1.2 withdrew the phrase "the most natural movement of the visions as they stand" because the chapter also admitted that neither reading was excluded and that both paid interpretive costs.

Why that rationale had to change. A viable rival reading does not by itself prevent one reading from being stronger. The resistance test found that the local grammar of Revelation 20:4-6 strongly favors the consecutive reading: the same verb in the same form governs both groups, "the rest of the dead" marks the first group as having been among the dead, until makes the second group's coming to life the delayed counterpart of the first, and John calls the first event a resurrection. Revelation 2:8 and 13:14 use the same verb for coming back to life after death.

The same test also found that the recapitulation reading had not been given its strongest structural and canonical evidence. Revelation 16:14 and 20:8 use the same gathering-for-battle language; Revelation 19:17-21 and 20:8-9 both draw on Ezekiel's Gog complex; Revelation 20:2 repeats the dragon's titles from 12:9; and John 6:40, John 12:48, Acts 24:15, and 1 Corinthians 15:54 place real pressure toward a single end-time resurrection-and-judgment complex. Revelation 20:8 also permits the purpose-limited reading of Satan's deception to be drawn from the same paragraph rather than imported wholly from outside it.

What Version 1.6 says instead. On the grammar of Revelation 20:4-6, the consecutive reading has the stronger case. On the structure of Revelation 12-20 and on the single-end texts, recapitulation has substantial counterevidence. LST continues to hold the future millennial interval as a defended synthesis, but it does not presently claim that the consecutive reading has the stronger case overall and it does not restore the phrase "most natural movement." The earlier withdrawal stands on a corrected rationale.


REVISION 16
Chapter 16, The Davidic Kingdom and the Return of Christ
The forward pointer to Chapter 17 and the retrospective correction of Revision 6
Effect: partially reverses Revision 6 by restoring a pointer to the temporal interval while refusing to smuggle public or administrative content into it

What stood after Revision 6. Version 1.2 removed the sentence "The argument for an intermediate public stage belongs in Chapter 17" and said that Chapter 17 did not hold such a stage and that nothing was waiting there to be shown.

Why it had to change. Chapter 17 plainly holds a future millennial interval before the final judgment as LST's present defended synthesis. Revision 6 had fused separate questions: whether an interval exists, whether the saints reign in it, whether that reign is public, whether it is earthly, and what territorial or administrative content it contains. The first two belong to Chapter 17's positive case; the others require further argument.

What Version 1.6 says instead. Chapter 16 again points to Chapter 17 for the argument for a future interval between Christ's appearing and the final judgment. The word public is not restored as shorthand for the interval. The pointer expressly says that Revelation 20 does not by itself supply territorial, sanctuary, sacrificial, or civil content. Chapter 15's narrower post-coming territorial proposal is cross-referenced at argued-possibility confidence rather than imported into the millennium by definition.


REVISION 17
Chapter 10, God's Revealed Will and the Universal Gospel Call
The two-wills distinction and the retrospective correction of Revision 8
Effect: reverses Revision 8's governing rationale and restores the distinction as a guard against reduction without claiming that it explains the metaphysical fit

What stood after Revision 8. Version 1.2 withdrew the claim that the preceptive/decretive distinction "prevents two reductions" and stated that a distinction which cannot be shown to cohere has not prevented anything.

Why that rationale had to change. The resistance test showed that the sentence confused two different tasks.
