<h1 align="center">LST Revision Resistance</h1>

## Purpose

Living Systematic Theology is fallible. Scripture is the final authority. A criticism, AI audit, or existing LST paragraph is not automatically a verdict.

## How a Proposed Revision Is Tested

1. Identify the exact existing claim, its confidence level, and proposed correction.
2. Examine the relevant biblical passages in their immediate literary, grammatical, historical, and canonical contexts.
3. Build the strongest biblical case for retaining the current formulation, including overlooked supporting texts.
4. Determine whether that case establishes the proposition, rather than merely showing that it is possible.
5. Build the strongest biblical case for revising the formulation, including the strongest opposing texts.
6. Judge both cases by the same standards: direct teaching versus inference, context before harmonization, verified source claims, and no argument from silence.
7. Distinguish a true doctrine with a weak argument from a doctrine Scripture does not sustain. Improve proof where appropriate; qualify, regrade, revise, or abandon a conclusion when necessary.
8. Explain the result in a public verdict, acknowledge unresolved objections, and identify what new evidence could change the outcome.
9. Preserve superseded governing documents, record significant approved changes, and verify the public release. AI assists research; the human maintainer controls official publication.

## What Every Public Adjudication Must Show

A reader should be able to understand and challenge the outcome **without trusting a hidden AI conversation**. The GitHub issue or a directly linked public verdict should disclose:

1. **The claim and provenance.** The precise current LST wording and confidence level, the challenged proposition, and the relevant public issue. Identify whether it was filed from the maintainer's account or another account; do **not** equate account ownership with who originally conceived the argument. Credit external source authors only when known.
2. **Review responsibility.** Name the human maintainer approving the public outcome, the material ways AI assisted (research, drafting, adversarial testing), and whether *genuinely independent external review* occurred. If it did not, say so. An AI instructed to argue against or defend LST is never independent peer review.
3. **The strongest evidence on both sides.** Present the challenger's actual arguments and best passages fairly, then the strongest biblical case for retaining LST. Both must receive contextual analysis, not just lists of citations.
4. **Evidence coverage and source provenance.** For each decisive passage or historical/lexical claim, show the relevant point, how it was tested, and what it supports or fails to establish. Cite materially used public scholarly or historical sources when possible; mark inaccessible, unchecked, or contested sources openly. Identify significant unanswered arguments rather than silently omitting them.
5. **Symmetric reasoning.** Identify what Scripture states, what follows by inference, what is merely compatible, and any assumptions introduced by either side. Do not turn a weaker proof text into an automatic doctrinal defeat or treat a possible reply as a demonstrated vindication.
6. **A falsifiable conclusion.** State the formal verdict, its reasons, the surviving objections, and what further biblical evidence or correction could change it. Explain any modification to the doctrine, wording, argument, or confidence grade—or explicitly state that nothing changed.
7. **An independently checkable change record.** Link the affected Systematic/Creed passages and Revision Record entry when a revision is made. Distinguish a proposed edit from one approved, saved, and verified in Drive and published to GitHub.
8. **The challenger’s opportunity to answer.** Link the issue's existing rebuttal and closure rules, state the applicable window and round count, and respond substantively to timely rebuttals. Silence does not establish theological truth.

**No automated rejection:** An AI-generated draft, internal test, or instruction to argue for the current LST position cannot itself decide a challenge. An adverse verdict must be justified by publicly inspectable evidence and approved by the human maintainer. This does not promise that every external scholar reviewed it or that human approval guarantees the conclusion is correct.

**Fair evidence map:** Ideally the public verdict identifies *challenge raised → passage/source examined → finding → remaining objection*, especially where the challenger supplied numerous substantive countertexts. If an essential point remains unexamined, label the outcome provisional or unresolved to that extent rather than claiming the point was refuted.

## Version 2.6 Confidence-Grading Clarification

Revision Resistance G1 was adjudicated in LST Version 2.6 ([Revision 36](LST_REVISION_RECORD.md#revision-36)). A claim directly and clearly taught by Scripture can be confessed even if supported by one decisive verse; the number of verses is not a mechanical grade. A technical proposition that requires inference remains synthesis unless the cumulative biblical case necessarily establishes it. Individual doctrinal grades remain open to further textual examination.

## Privacy

The public methodology and GitHub challenge rulings are examinable. Private Facebook conversations and LST Thread Ledgers are not shared or synchronized to GitHub.

To test a proposed correction yourself, copy the [Official LST AI Auditor Prompt](LST_OFFICIAL_AI_AUDITOR_PROMPT.md) and supply the current formulation and your strongest biblical challenge. This optional AI exercise cannot approve or exclude a public issue.
