<h1 align="center">1. Purpose and Safeguards</h1>
<p align="center"><em>Biblical truth before institutional loyalty, with clear safeguards against bias</em></p>
<p align="center">✦ <strong>Scripture governs</strong> · Evidence matters · Every claim remains testable ✦</p>

**Status: proposed independent-review pilot, not current enacted policy.**

## What we are trying to achieve

LST seeks the theology best supported by Scripture, as far as fallible readers can tell. It must not become an unexamined mixture of every plausible theory. Neither may an entrenched doctrine survive merely because it is already written in the Systematic.

A proposed correction faces the best *positive* biblical case for retaining the current formulation. That is **Revision Resistance**. This is a **test of evidence**, not a rule that the current doctrine must win. A successful defense shows both that a criticism fails **and** that the old teaching positively deserves its stated level of confidence. A failed criticism alone does not prove that the old teaching is true.

## Separate the jobs

An adversarial examiner strengthens the biblical case *against* the challenged claim. A dedicated **ChatGPT defender** strengthens the biblical case *for* the existing claim. The independent adjudicator weighs them under identical interpretive rules. The independent verifier then tries to break the adjudicator's reasoning. None may substitute the popularity of a reading, an AI consensus, or its assigned advocacy role for actual interpretation.

The defender is intentionally one-sided in *assignment*; the adjudicator and verifier must not be. This is more honest than pretending that a single LST-tuned AI is simultaneously advocate and neutral referee.

## Bias is limited, not abolished

No commercially trained AI is provably neutral. Fresh sessions may still share training distributions, doctrinal assumptions, persuasive-writing preferences, or correlated mistakes. Fixed public prompts can themselves embed questionable methodological judgments. To reduce—not deny—these risks:

- Use fresh sessions and separate models for adjudication and verification, with no personal theological memory or custom instructions where feasible.
- First anonymize “LST” and “challenger” as Position A and Position B for the judge where that would not erase relevant context; then reveal the current LST claim and confidence for the final compatibility assessment.
- Give the judge both full cases and the actual decisive texts, not a T.A.I.T.-authored summary of the critic's case.
- When practical, repeat an independent comparison with the positions reversed to check order effects.
- Require source-level evidence, explicit uncertainty, a candid record of inaccessible materials, and public exposure of disagreements.
- Never treat four agreeing models as four independent eyewitnesses to truth.

## What a human does—and does not do

The owner, Ken Kibbey, controls GitHub/Drive access, privacy, publication, scheduling, historical archives, and the authorization of actual changes. These duties cannot honestly be called absent human control. **The owner is not supposed to privately replace the adjudicator's scriptural conclusions with his preferred theology.** If he disputes the outcome, he should publish his separate objection and biblical grounds for review.

The proposed neutral AI can assess doctrine without a human acting as the secret theological referee, but its judgment is still a **fallible analysis**, not an inspired verdict or an autonomous authorization to alter someone else's accounts. If the owner declines to publish or implement a recommendation, the fact and reasons must remain visible rather than being passed off as the AI's verdict.

## Boundary of the pilot

This document does not retroactively convert previous GitHub issues into independent four-AI rulings. The currently published CONTRIBUTING procedure applies until an approved and verified change to the governing Drive documents and GitHub public rules. Neither privately held debate ledgers nor private conversations are inputs for the public adjudicator unless explicitly authorized and appropriate to disclose. Public claims should be independently supportable from the issue, Scripture, and identified source materials.
