**Grade definitions** (`LST_GLOSSARY.md`):

## A2 — Faithful restatement of the original challenge

**Provenance of the challenge.** The issue was filed by the project owner and states it was "prepared with ChatGPT in the LST Project Engineer thread." It describes itself as the **initial case**, not a completed Prompt A report. Below, "the issue" means that original text. Arguments I add are labeled **AI-ADDED (Claude)**.

**What the issue asks.** Whether the *combined positive biblical evidence* now warrants moving eternal conscious punishment (ECP) from argued possibility to defended synthesis, **without** claiming any single verse settles it and **without** reinstating arguments the earlier sustained challenges defeated.

**Its four positive pillars:**
- **P1 — Matthew 25:41, 46.** Eternal punishment stands in parallel with eternal life; condemned humans go into the eternal fire prepared for the devil and his angels. Does the setting support an *enduring experience* rather than a temporary experience with an everlasting result? Does conditional immortality (CI) import a distinction between the two uses of "eternal" that the passage does not require?
- **P2 — Revelation 14:9–11.** Direct depiction of human torment and no rest day or night. **Concession:** "forever and ever" grammatically modifies the smoke, not the no-rest clause. The question is whether the wider picture contributes more than LST now assigns.
- **P3 — Revelation 20:10–15; 21:8.** The devil, beast, and false prophet are tormented forever; condemned humans enter the same lake, whose human outcome is called the second death. **Concession:** identical location alone does not establish identical experience.
- **P4 — Daniel 12:2; 2 Thessalonians 1:9.** Everlasting disgrace/contempt and eternal destruction "from the presence of the Lord" may contribute; test whether either supports enduring *consciousness*.

**Counterevidence the issue requires to be answered:** Matthew 10:28; John 3:16; Romans 6:23; 2 Thessalonians 1:9; Jude 7 / 2 Peter 2:6; Isaiah 66:24 / Mark 9:43–48; Revelation 19:3; 20:14–15. Neither side may redefine *death*, *destroy*, *perish*, nor assume those words settle the question.

**Constraints it places on itself:** It must directly address (1) the grammar of Rev 14:11, (2) the distinction between eternal *consequence* and everlasting *consciousness*, (3) Jude 7, and (4) the human "second death."

**Decision questions:** (1) Does ECP have stronger positive warrant than CI? (2) Does it reach DS or remain AP? (3) Has LST 2.8 weighted all the controlling texts appropriately? (4) Should the earlier reduction be retained, qualified, or reversed on *newly demonstrated* warrant?

**Falsification standard (issue's own words, in substance):** if the passages do not establish the distinctive inference to endless conscious human experience strongly enough, recommend NOT SUSTAINED, even if everlasting punishment remains plausible; if promotion is supported, recommend only the smallest justified increase.

**One feature of the issue to note:** it lists 2 Thessalonians 1:9 on both the positive side (P4) and the counterevidence side. That is not an error; the verse genuinely cuts both ways (see O5).

**O1 — Matthew 25:41, 46: the fire defined by its first occupants, and a punishment that is undergone.**
*Origin:* ORIGINAL (P1). Lexical and Danielic refinements are **AI-ADDED (Claude)**.

*Stated.* The cursed "go" into "the eternal fire prepared for the devil and his angels" (τὸ πῦρ τὸ αἰώνιον τὸ ἡτοιμασμένον τῷ διαβόλῳ καὶ τοῖς ἀγγέλοις αὐτοῦ, 25:41); "these will go away into eternal punishment (κόλασιν αἰώνιον), but the righteous into eternal life" (25:46). The judgment scene is the Son of Man enthroned with his angels (25:31).

*Argument.*
1. Jesus defines the humans' destination by reference to the devil's fire, and 25:34 sets up a deliberate counterpart: the kingdom "prepared for you." The only canonical description of what the devil's final fire does to him is everlasting torment (Rev 20:10). The issue's question stands: CI must supply a difference in the fire's effect on humans that Matthew does not mention.
2. **AI-ADDED (lexical usage):** κόλασις occurs only twice in the NT. Its other use, 1 John 4:18 ("fear has κόλασιν"), names something *felt*. That is modest evidence that the noun denotes punishment undergone rather than only a punitive result.
3. **AI-ADDED (canonical):** 25:46's two-destiny shape, with "eternal life" as one pole, matches Daniel 12:2 (Theodotion: οὗτοι εἰς ζωὴν αἰώνιον καὶ οὗτοι εἰς ὀνειδισμὸν καὶ εἰς αἰσχύνην αἰώνιον), within a Danielic Son-of-Man scene. If Matthew draws on Daniel 12:2, the negative pole is a destiny that **resurrected persons** enter, not corpses (see O6).

*Inferred.* That the punishment in 25:46 is an ongoing experience co-extensive with eternal life.

*Weaknesses (stated plainly).* Point 1 is the shared-destination link #15 judged insufficient; Jude 7 shows "eternal fire" can consume. For point 2, Hebrews 9:12 (αἰωνίαν λύτρωσιν, "eternal redemption") and 6:2 (κρίματος αἰωνίου, "eternal judgment") show that -σις / judgment nouns with αἰώνιος can name a once-for-all act with everlasting effect. One other occurrence of κόλασις is thin evidence. Point 3 may cut the other way: Daniel's term for the wicked's outcome is the one Isaiah 66:24 uses for corpses (O6).

*Strength:* Real pressure; **not compelling by itself.** LST already calls this "the strongest pressure."

**O2 — Revelation's torment formula: four elements, applied to humans and to the devil's lake.**
*Origin:* ORIGINAL (P2, P3) in substance. The formula-convergence argument is **AI-ADDED (Claude)**.

*Stated.*
- 14:10–11 (beast-worshipers): βασανισθήσεται ἐν πυρὶ καὶ θείῳ ("will be tormented with fire and sulfur") … ὁ καπνὸς τοῦ βασανισμοῦ αὐτῶν εἰς αἰῶνας αἰώνων ἀναβαίνει, καὶ οὐκ ἔχουσιν ἀνάπαυσιν ἡμέρας καὶ νυκτός.
- 20:10 (devil, beast, false prophet in "the lake of fire and sulfur"): βασανισθήσονται ἡμέρας καὶ νυκτὸς εἰς τοὺς αἰῶνας τῶν αἰώνων.
- 21:8 (humans): "their portion (τὸ μέρος αὐτῶν) in the lake burning with fire and sulfur, which is the second death."

*Argument.* This is more than shared location. Every element of the 20:10 torment formula appears in 14:10–11 for humans: (i) the verb βασανίζω, (ii) "fire and sulfur," (iii) "day and night," (iv) the eternity phrase. And 21:8 describes the humans' "portion" in a lake with the same "fire and sulfur." John uses one vocabulary for human and diabolical torment, and the place where that vocabulary is given its explicit duration is the same place 21:8 assigns as the human portion. LST also says elsewhere (Chapter 23, Isaiah 65 paragraph) that it "refuses narrowing the no-rest clause of Revelation 14:11 to a completed destruction," which concedes the human unrest is presented as ongoing at the time the eternal smoke is rising.

*Inferred.* That the formula's duration element, explicit in 20:10, belongs to the human torment as well.

*Weaknesses (stated plainly).* The decisive objection from #6 still stands: in 14:11 John places the eternity phrase on the *smoke*, not on the torment verb, while in 20:10 he places it on the verb. A formula-convergence argument cannot erase that difference, and it partly repackages the move #6 defeated. Babylon too receives βασανισμός (18:7, 10), its "smoke of her burning" (18:9, 18) rises after she is burned, and 19:3 repeats the eternal-smoke formula after she "will be found no more." Also, 14:10's scene is "before the holy angels and the Lamb," and is not explicitly located in the lake. The LST remark about not narrowing the no-rest clause concerns the clause's present scope, not its duration.

*Strength:* The **best available positive form** of P2–P3, and stronger than "same location." **Still does not supply a human-directed duration clause.**

**O3 — In Revelation, the beast "goes to destruction" and is tormented forever.**
*Origin:* **AI-ADDED (Claude).** Not in the issue.

*Stated.* The beast "is about to come up out of the abyss and go to destruction" (εἰς ἀπώλειαν ὑπάγει, 17:8; repeated 17:11). The beast is then "thrown alive" into the lake (19:20), and 20:10 says the beast is tormented day and night forever and ever.

*Argument.* Within one book, John predicates ἀπώλεια ("destruction, perdition") of a subject whose lake-end he describes as everlasting torment. So in John's own idiom, destruction vocabulary does **not** by itself entail cessation. This directly weakens the counter-pressure from John 3:16 (ἀπόληται), Philippians 3:19 (ἀπώλεια), Hebrews 10:39, and 2 Peter 3:7. It answers the issue's instruction neither to redefine "destroy" nor to assume it settles the question: here the text itself shows the word's range.

*Weaknesses (stated plainly).* This is **neutralizing evidence, not positive evidence for human ECP.** And it is double-edged. Revelation's beast comes from Daniel 7, where "the beast was slain, and its body destroyed and given to the burning of fire" (Dan 7:11; Aramaic וְהוּבַד גִּשְׁמַהּ; Theodotion ἀπώλετο). Daniel explains the beasts as kings and kingdoms (7:17, 23). If the beast is a corporate power, then "tormented forever" applied to it may be imagery for a destroyed empire, and that would weaken the literal force of 20:10 itself.

*Strength:* **Moderate** as a defeater of the inference "destroy ⇒ cease to exist"; **cannot carry promotion.**

**O4 — Are the beast and the false prophet human individuals?**
*Origin:* **AI-ADDED (Claude).**

*Stated.* "It is the number of a man" (ἀριθμὸς γὰρ ἀνθρώπου ἐστίν, 13:18); the beast is "an eighth" king (17:11); the false prophet "performed the signs" and deceived people (19:20); both were "thrown alive" (ζῶντες) into the lake (19:20). Then 20:10 says "where also the beast and the false prophet [are]," and all are tormented forever.

*Argument.* If the beast and false prophet are individual human beings, 20:10 is a **human-directed** everlasting-torment clause. That would remove LST's distinction that the explicit duration clause names only non-human subjects (Creed: "LST no longer rests the consciousness of human punishment on extending verse 10 to persons it does not name"). It would show at least that the lake's everlasting torment is something human beings can undergo.

*Weaknesses (stated plainly).* The identification is contested within Revelation itself: 21:17 uses "a man's measure, which is an angel's," so "of a man" can mean ordinary human reckoning. Daniel 7:17, 23 is corporate, and the sea-beast has seven heads and ten horns (13:1). Even granting two individual humans, it would not show that "anyone not found written in the book of life" (20:15) shares their experience rather than the second death named for them. The "where also" persistence argument depends on a consecutive millennial reading, which LST holds only as an argued possibility (Chapter 17).

*Strength:* **Low to moderate.** If Prompt C accepted the individual-human identification, this would be the single most consequential new point in the case.

**O5 — 2 Thessalonians 1:9 echoes Isaiah 2 verbatim: persons excluded from the face of the Lord.**
*Origin:* ORIGINAL (P4). The Isaiah echo and usage argument is **AI-ADDED (Claude).**

*Stated.* οἵτινες δίκην τίσουσιν ὄλεθρον αἰώνιον ἀπὸ προσώπου τοῦ κυρίου καὶ ἀπὸ τῆς δόξης τῆς ἰσχύος αὐτοῦ (2 Thess 1:9). LXX Isaiah 2:10 (and 2:19): … ἀπὸ προσώπου τοῦ φόβου κυρίου καὶ ἀπὸ τῆς δόξης τῆς ἰσχύος αὐτοῦ, ὅταν ἀναστῇ θραῦσαι τὴν γῆν. Paul's phrase reproduces Isaiah's except for "the fear," and his "when he comes" (ὅταν ἔλθῃ, 1:10) mirrors Isaiah's "when he rises."

*Argument.* In Isaiah the phrase describes **living people hiding** in rocks and caves *from* the Lord's face and glory. That favors reading Paul's ἀπό as separation/exclusion of an existing subject from the presence, rather than "destruction that comes from" the presence. Paul's other uses show ὄλεθρος need not mean annihilation of the subject: in 1 Corinthians 5:5 the man is handed over "for the ὄλεθρος of the flesh, so that the spirit may be saved," and in 1 Thessalonians 5:3 ὄλεθρος is something that comes upon people who "will not escape." LST currently lists 2 Thessalonians 1:9 only among the destruction texts (counter-pressure). At minimum, this shows it belongs on both sides.

*Weaknesses (stated plainly).* ἀπό can be source or cause. "Eternal ruin" is the plain sense of ὄλεθρον αἰώνιον. Isaiah's hiding people are not said to persist forever. 1 Corinthians 5:5 is remedial and concerns "the flesh." The verse remains ambiguous on duration of consciousness.

*Strength:* **Modest.** It corrects a one-sided placement more than it proves (c).

**O6 — Daniel 12:2: resurrected persons, not corpses, receive "everlasting abhorrence."**
*Origin:* ORIGINAL (P4). Textual details are **AI-ADDED (Claude).**

*Stated (MT).* "Many of those who sleep in the dust of the ground will awake, these to everlasting life (לְחַיֵּי עוֹלָם), and these to reproaches, to everlasting abhorrence (לַחֲרָפוֹת לְדִרְאוֹן עוֹלָם)." דִּרְאוֹן is in construct with עוֹלָם. Theodotion renders "to reproach and to eternal shame (αἰσχύνην αἰώνιον)"; Old Greek adds "to dispersion" between them.

*Argument.* Daniel takes the rare noun דֵּרָאוֹן (elsewhere only Isa 66:24) and applies it not to corpses but to people **raised** to receive it, set in strict parallel with "everlasting life." Raising people for an everlasting state of shame makes most natural sense if they exist to bear it. Both Greek translators chose a word for shame, which is usually something the subject undergoes. Combined with O1.3, this offers a Danielic line from Daniel 12:2 to Matthew 25:46.

*Weaknesses (stated plainly).* In Isaiah 66:24 the דֵּרָאוֹן is what "all flesh" feels toward the dead; the LXX there renders "a sight to all flesh." That makes the word observer-relative, and "everlasting abhorrence" can be an everlasting verdict or memory, not an everlasting experience. "Reproaches" is likewise objective. **G3 already weighed this and ruled it insufficient for regrading**, and Issue #35 does not engage G3. The only possibly fresh element is the Daniel → Matthew 25:46 link, and that link can transmit Daniel's ambiguity rather than resolve it.

*Strength:* **Real but limited.** Strengthens the punishment case (as G3 said); does not complete (c).

**O7 — In Revelation, "death" does not by its lexeme mean non-existence.**
*Origin:* **AI-ADDED (Claude)**, responding to the issue's counter-evidence list.

*Stated.* Those who suffered the first death are presented as conscious souls who cry out (6:9–10); death and Hades "gave up the dead in them" for judgment (20:13).

*Argument.* John's first death leaves persons in existence. So the name "second death" (2:11; 20:6, 14; 21:8) does not, merely as a word, establish cessation of being. That answers the use of "second death" as a lexical trump card against ECP.

*Weaknesses (stated plainly).* 20:5 says "the rest of the dead did not live (οὐκ ἔζησαν) until…," so "death" in John still means loss of life. The first death is followed by resurrection; the second has none. And in 20:14 the lake receives Death and Hades, which then cease: "death will be no more" (21:4). That is the strongest single verse for LST's position (see A4.4).

*Strength:* **Neutralizer only.**

**O9 — Calibration parity with other defended syntheses.**
*Origin:* **AI-ADDED (Claude)**, methodological.

*Argument.* By the Glossary, DS means "supported by multiple biblical passages and careful reasoning, but not stated in full as a single direct biblical proposition." ECP has multiple supporting passages (Matt 25:41, 46; Rev 14:10–11; 20:10 with 21:8; Dan 12:2) and an articulated inference. LST keeps eternal generation at DS even though "no verse states the technical proposition" and Revision 36 recorded a real lexical countercase (μονογενής; Psalm 2:7 as appointment). The test applied in #15, that the distinctive inference must be *completed*, may be stricter than the test applied to other DS doctrines.

*Weaknesses (stated plainly).* The more apt LST precedent is Chapter 17 (millennium): where **both** rival readings have real positive support, LST grades **both** AP. CI has positive texts of its own, not just objections to ECP: Jude 7 with 2 Peter 2:6 ("reduced to ashes… an example of what is coming"); Malachi 3:19, 21 MT (4:1, 3 EV), where the wicked are stubble that leaves "neither root nor branch" and become "ashes under the soles of your feet"; Matthew 3:12 (chaff "burned up" with unquenchable fire); Matthew 10:28; Isaiah 66:24 (corpses); and the human "second death." Eternal generation faces no rival reading with that kind of explicit textual support. Parity therefore probably cuts toward **retaining** AP.

*Strength:* **Weak** for promotion; worth recording because it tests whether LST's grading is consistent.
