## 3. Evaluation of Grok's comparative reasoning, and the error in my own

### 3.1 The standard that must govern

The owner's instruction states the right test: compare both interpretations under the **same evidentiary standard**. Direct statements must not be discounted because they use destruction language, and figurative passages must not be promoted because they describe duration.

Sorting the key texts by **kind** rather than by **which side they favor** gives this:

| Kind of text | Natural reading favors ECP | Natural reading favors CI |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct propositional statements about final destiny** | Matthew 25:46 ("go away into eternal punishment," κόλασιν αἰώνιον, set against "eternal life") | 2 Thessalonians 1:9 ("pay the penalty, eternal destruction," δίκην … ὄλεθρον αἰώνιον); Matthew 10:28 (God able to "destroy soul and body in Gehenna"); Romans 6:23 (death against eternal life); John 3:16 (perish against eternal life) |
| **Prophetic or visionary depictions** | Revelation 14:10–11; Revelation 20:10 with 20:15; Daniel 12:2 (ambiguous) | Revelation 20:14 with 21:4 (Death and Hades cease in the lake); Malachi 3:19–21; Isaiah 66:24; Hebrews 10:27 (fire about to consume) |
| **Parabolic or exemplary figures** | — | Matthew 3:12 (chaff burned up); 2 Peter 2:6 (cities reduced to ashes as an example) |
| **Two-edged** | Mark 9:43–48; Jude 7; the beast's ἀπώλεια with its torment (Revelation 17:8, 11; 20:10) | (same texts) |

**Matthew 25:46 and 2 Thessalonians 1:9 are exact counterparts.** **AI-ADDED (Claude, final verification).** Verified in SBLGNT:
- Both describe the **same event**: the Lord's coming with his angels (Matthew 25:31, "when the Son of Man comes … and all the angels with him"; 2 Thessalonians 1:7, 10, "with his mighty angels … when he comes").
- Both describe a **two-group outcome**.
- Both state the wicked's lot as **αἰώνιος + a noun of penalty**: κόλασις in Matthew; δίκη explained by ὄλεθρος, in apposition, in Paul.
- Paul's sentence states directly that the eternal **penalty is** "eternal destruction."

ECP must read ὄλεθρος as ruin rather than cessation; Paul's own uses in 1 Corinthians 5:5 and 1 Thessalonians 5:3 allow this. CI must read κόλασις as a penalty with an everlasting result; Hebrews 9:12 shows the pattern. **Each side's best direct text is matched by a direct text on the other side, and each reading of the other side's text has a precedent within Scripture.** No prior report, including mine, set these two texts side by side as counterparts.

### 3.2 The error in my addendum's S7

My addendum said ECP has "the stronger direct testimony about the final state," and that CI has "the broader testimony of destruction imagery." The issue notice repeated this. **That classification applied two standards:**

1. It counted **Revelation 14 and 20**, which are apocalyptic visions full of symbolic figures (beast, lake, smoke), as "direct" testimony for ECP.
2. It counted **Matthew 10:28, 2 Thessalonians 1:9, Romans 6:23 and John 3:16**, which are direct propositional statements, as "imagery" for CI, alongside the genuinely figurative Malachi 3 and Matthew 3:12.

Under a single standard, as in the table above, the direct propositional texts are **at least evenly divided**. The CI side has more of them. ECP's advantage lies in **explicit duration language within visionary passages** (Revelation 14:11; 20:10). CI's advantage lies in **explicit language about the nature of the outcome within direct statements** (destroy, perish, death, destruction).

Each side is direct on one question and indirect on the other:
- ECP's texts speak most directly to **duration**.
- CI's texts speak most directly to **what the outcome is**.

Scripture does not tell the reader which question governs the other.

**Correction.** The "modest but contestable edge for ECP" stated in addendum S7, and in my Issue #35 notice of October 22, 02:03 UTC, is **withdrawn**. It depended on the inconsistent classification just described, not on any textual fact.

**Why I am withdrawing it.** I found this specific error by re-sorting the evidence under one standard (table, section 3.1). The finding that Grok reached a similar conclusion played no part, and agreement among reviewers is not evidence. Had the consistent standard still favored ECP, I would have kept the finding and recorded it as a disagreement with Grok. The textual corrections that accompanied it stand. They reduce the force LST gives four counter-texts, but **reducing the other side's counter-evidence is not positive evidence for ECP**. That distinction was correctly drawn in my V6.4, and S7 lost sight of it.

### 3.3 Grok's comparative reasoning

Grok's core conclusion is that the comparative evidence does not establish a clear preference: ECP is stronger in direct parallel testimony, CI broader in destruction language, and Scripture does not say which governs. That conclusion is **correct, and on my re-examination better supported than my S7**.

Grok's §4 and §5, however, partly share my S7 inconsistency:
- §4 describes CI's evidence as "ordinary destruction, perish, and consumption language," without noting that much of it consists of direct statements.
- §5 proposes a clarification that "the direct parallel of Matthew 25:46 and the shared-lake imagery supply stronger propositional pressure for ECP than the ordinary force of destruction language supplies for CI."

That §5 sentence:
- calls a visionary image ("shared-lake imagery") "propositional pressure";
- ranks Matthew 25:46 above 2 Thessalonians 1:9 and Matthew 10:28 without showing why;
- conflicts with Grok's own §5 instruction that the clarification be made "without asserting a preference," and with its §6 finding that neither model has clearly stronger warrant.

**This is a drafting defect, not an error in Grok's conclusion.** It matters only if the §5 sentence were adopted as Chapter 23 wording. It should not be (see section 6).

### 3.4 Confidence standards and the type of correction

- **Confidence standards.** Grok applies LST's standards correctly. Both models have a positive case and neither is demonstrated, so ARGUED POSSIBILITY is correct for both. DEFENDED SYNTHESIS fails for ECP because the cumulative inference does not answer CI's direct texts (V6.4).
- **Correction versus preference.** Grok correctly separates the three:
  - retention is warranted;
  - the passage-level and wording clarifications are presentation improvements;
  - a stated preference is not required.
  
  I agree, with one refinement. Of the passage corrections, the four that fix **inaccurate or overstated descriptions of the text** (section 6, item 2) are corrections of accuracy, not optional preferences.
