### S5. Independent evaluation of the October 8 arguments

Each argument was tested against the texts I had already checked for the original report, plus new checks where marked. The earlier conclusions were not assumed correct.

**S5.1 Matthew 25:46 "coordinates duration."** The audit argues that if the life is everlasting, the punishment is "presented as everlasting in the same sense, unless a contextual reason splits them."

This is **true but does not reach the disputed point**. CI does not split the sense of αἰώνιος. It agrees both destinies are everlasting, and construes κόλασις as a punishment whose result is everlasting (compare Hebrews 9:12, verified in the original V2.2 item 1). The audit's falsification test asks for proof that the parallel does not coordinate duration, which CI never claims. So the test does not engage CI's position. **Verdicts C answered this correctly** with Hebrews 6:2 and 9:12. The audit's point is accurate as far as it goes. It does not decide the case.

**S5.2 "Destroy-language is compatible with both models and therefore cannot level them."**
- *Partly verified.* The beast's ἀπώλεια (Revelation 17:8, 11) alongside its everlasting torment (20:10) shows that John's destruction vocabulary does not by itself entail cessation (original V2.2 item 3).
- *But the argument is applied to only one side.* By the same logic, Matthew 25:46 is compatible with both models (Hebrews 9:12), so it too "cannot level them."
- The audit applies the compatibility test to CI's texts and not to ECP's. That is a **reasoning flaw** in the audit. Prompt A's O3 had already made the fair version of this point; the original Prompt D report verified it.

**S5.3 Mark 9:43–48: the imagery applied to persons.** **AI-checked (Claude, Prompt D supplement).** I verified this in SBLGNT, the Byzantine text, and Nestle 1904.
- In 9:43, 45, and 47 a person "having two hands … two feet … two eyes" goes away or is thrown "into Gehenna, into the unquenchable fire." These are whole, embodied persons, not corpses.
- In 9:48, "where their worm does not die and the fire is not quenched" uses **present** tense (οὐ τελευτᾷ, οὐ σβέννυται). Isaiah 66:24 LXX has the future (οὐ τελευτήσει, οὐ σβεσθήσεται).
- Isaiah's scene is observers viewing the **corpses** (פִּגְרֵי; LXX κῶλα) of rebels. Jesus' saying describes the **place** into which living persons are cast.

*Finding.* The audit's point is **correct and material**, and it was **not weighed** by Prompt A (A4.5), Prompt B (B2), Grok (J4), or my original report (V2.2 item 8; V6.4(d)). All of these treated Mark 9:48 simply as a corpse scene. LST's sentence, "Mark 9 takes its undying-worm and unquenchable-fire language from that corpse scene," is accurate as to source but incomplete as to application.

The adaptation is still **two-edged**:
- ECP can read it as a lasting condition of persons in a place with undying agents.
- CI can read the worm and fire as agents of consumption. This fits Matthew 10:28, "destroy soul and body in Gehenna," and Matthew 3:12, where the unquenchable fire burns the chaff up.

So Mark 9:48 moves from a **CI-side** text to a **neutral, two-edged** text. It does not become positive ECP duration evidence.

**S5.4 Revelation 14:11's no-rest clause.** The audit says equal standing would be restored if the no-rest clause were shown to be "bounded by the judgment scene." This **shifts the burden**. The clause carries "day and night" (unbroken continuity) but no eternity phrase. Grammatically it neither states nor excludes endlessness. LST already refuses to narrow it to a completed destruction (Ch. 23, Isaiah 65 paragraph), and the original report verified this. The point **supports ECP's AP standing**; it does not demonstrate endless duration.

**S5.5 "Second death" as a possible penal state.** Consistent with, and strengthened by, the original report's finding that Revelation 20:14 names **the lake itself** the second death (original V2.2 item 3). Note that the October 8 audit, the October 8 verdicts, LST v2.8, Prompt B, and Grok **all** repeat the imprecise phrasing that John calls "the human outcome" the second death.

**S5.6 Verdicts C: "a shared category is not a declaration of equal likelihood."** **Correct as a principle.** But the current Chapter 23, Creed II.5, and the Labels Guide never say so. Readers can therefore infer equality. Issue #35's decision question 1 asked directly whether ECP has stronger warrant than CI. **Only Prompt A answered it** ("close to balanced"):

### S6. One further check prompted by the supplement

**AI-checked (Claude, Prompt D supplement).** Hebrews 10:27, which the original report added as an uncited CI text, verbally echoes LXX Isaiah 26:11: "fire will devour the adversaries" (πῦρ τοὺς ὑπεναντίους ἔδεται, matching Hebrews' πυρὸς … ἐσθίειν … τοὺς ὑπεναντίους). Isaiah 26:14 then says of those oppressors that "the dead will not live … you destroyed them and wiped out all memory of them."

Two limits apply. That oracle concerns historical oppressors, and Isaiah 26:19 contrasts "your dead shall live." So the echo gives Hebrews 10:27 consumption language with a **destruction context**, but **modest** weight.

Hebrews 10:28–29 also calls the apostate's punishment (τιμωρία) "worse" than death without mercy under Moses. ECP can read "worse than death" as more than cessation. CI can read it as the second death beyond the first. This is two-edged and low in weight. It is recorded so later examiners do not miss it.

## Part 3 — Effect on the Issue #35 conclusions

### S7. Issue #35 decision question 1: does ECP have stronger positive warrant than CI?

**AI-ADDED (Claude, Prompt D supplement). This is my independent answer, not the October 8 audit's.**

**Where ECP is stronger: direct statements about the condition after judgment.**
- Matthew 25:46: a dominical, propositional sentence about the destinies of the two groups.
- Revelation 14:10–11: human torment, "smoke **of their torment**" rising forever, no rest day and night.
- Revelation 20:10 read with 20:14–15: the lake is the second death, it is where everlasting torment is stated, and condemned humans are cast into it.
- Daniel 12:2: resurrected persons raised to everlasting reproach.

Several texts LST cites against ECP carry **less** force than Chapter 23 gives them. This was verified in the original report and here:
- "second death," read as a distinctively human label;
- Jude 7, called "decisive";
- 2 Peter 2:6's "what is coming," which depends on a variant reading;
- Mark 9:48, read as a corpse scene.

**Where CI is stronger: the larger body of destruction and consumption language, much of it about what happens at the judgment.**
- Malachi 3:19–21: stubble, ashes underfoot.
- Matthew 3:12: chaff burned up with unquenchable fire.
- Matthew 10:28: destroy soul and body.
- Hebrews 10:27: fire about to consume, echoing Isaiah 26:11.
- 2 Peter 2:6: reduced to ashes.
- Romans 6:23 and John 3:16: death or perishing as the opposite of eternal life.
- 2 Thessalonians 1:9: eternal destruction.

CI also has **the one in-text demonstration that the lake's effect differs by subject**: Death and Hades cease (Revelation 20:14; 21:4).

**Each side's best texts can be answered from within Scripture:**
- Matthew 25:46 by the pattern of Hebrews 9:12;
- Revelation 14:11 by Isaiah 34:9–10 and Babylon (Revelation 18:7–19:3);
- the destruction vocabulary by the beast's ἀπώλεια together with its torment (17:8, 11; 20:10).

**Finding.**
- ECP has the **stronger direct testimony about the final state**. CI has the **broader testimony of destruction imagery**.
- Scripture does not itself say which kind of testimony should govern.
- On my weighing, ECP holds a **modest but contestable edge**, larger than LST's present wording conveys, because Chapter 23 cites four counter-texts at more than their verified weight.
- I **cannot verify this edge as determinate**. It depends on a weighting judgment (direct final-state statements against more numerous destruction images) that a reasonable examiner could make the other way.
- It **does not approach DEFENDED SYNTHESIS**. The original V6.4 tests (a)–(d) remain unmet.
