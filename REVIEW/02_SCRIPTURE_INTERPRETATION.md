<h1 align="center">2. How Scripture Is Interpreted</h1>
<p align="center"><em>Seven complementary stages of responsible biblical interpretation</em></p>
<p align="center">✦ <strong>Scripture governs</strong> · Evidence matters · Every claim remains testable ✦</p>

Every role uses the **same grammatical, historical, literary, and canonical interpretive method**. These are complementary safeguards, not four competing authorities. The method is open to better evidence and is itself open to public biblical criticism.

## The seven ordered stages

### 1. Textual basis

Identify the passage, translation, textual witnesses where relevant, significant variants, and *which sources were actually examined*. No invented Hebrew/Greek, manuscripts, manuscript variants, scholarship, or unverifiable claims of research. If evidence is missing, identify the limitation.

### 2. Grammar and word usage

Examine actual wording, syntax, clauses, verbal forms, discourse connections, and the *local* usage of disputed words. Words can carry multiple meanings. Related words are not automatically identical; dictionaries list possible senses rather than deciding the one a passage uses. A theological conclusion cannot rest on an asserted lexical meaning without contextual proof.

### 3. Historical setting

Ask what an original audience could reasonably understand in its linguistic, cultural, institutional, historical, and covenantal setting. Ancient literature, inscriptions, and modern scholarship can correct mistaken inherited readings. **The method does not presume that all ancient evidence is available today.** Nor does a general historical custom override a particular author's argument without demonstration.

### 4. Literary setting

Identify whether the writing is narrative, poetry, prophecy, wisdom, a letter, parable, or apocalypse. Examine the immediate paragraph, larger discourse, full book, audience, and rhetorical purpose. Respect figures and types without making the concrete words disappear.

### 5. Local communicated meaning

Before fitting the passage into a theological system, state what it actually **says**; what the argument **strongly implies**; what is **possible**; and what it **does not establish**. Work the strongest contextual rival reading and any genuine textual pressure. Do not dodge the present passage by quoting a convenient verse elsewhere.

### 6. Canonical interpretation

Interpret Scripture together: how does the rest of the biblical canon, including Christ and the apostles' inspired use of earlier Scripture, bear on this question? Later revelation may explain, extend, fulfill, or transform earlier revelation **where the biblical argument demonstrates it**. Do not erase the earlier author's true meaning, or make the whole Bible silent about a passage that later revelation actually clarifies.

### 7. Theological synthesis and confidence

Only now ask what the whole body of evidence supports, including counterevidence and how an interpretation affects other doctrines. Distinguish **directly confessed teaching**, **defended synthesis**, **argued possibility**, and **not demonstrated**. A theory being logically compatible with Scripture is not the same as Scripture positively teaching it. Neither a single clear verse nor a multi-text biblical argument is excluded in advance: the real issue is whether the passages actually sustain the claimed proposition.

## What about the Holy Spirit?

LST affirms the Spirit's work in understanding and obeying Scripture (1 Corinthians 2:12–16) and the duty to test claims (Acts 17:11; 1 Thessalonians 5:19–21). No reviewer may claim that the Spirit personally certified that AI's reading. Spiritual understanding does not make the written author's words, historical setting, and contextual argument disposable.

## Example of the method's self-correction

If someone argues that ancient `pistis` carries allegiance or that `charis` operates in gift/reciprocity relationships, that is **historical-language evidence to examine, not something our method prohibits**. The test is whether the reading explains the exact context, including Romans 4's wage/debt and trust distinction, James 2's relationship of faith and works, and relevant Pauline gift passages. Both a traditional and a novel gloss can fail this test. We do not settle the answer simply by choosing a theologian's name or a modern dictionary entry.

## What would overturn an interpretation?

A controlling text that the interpretation contradicts; a demonstrably erroneous premise; an omitted context that changes the argument; a source-critical correction; or a stronger cumulative biblical case. Conversely, a weak proof text may require improving an argument without reversing a positively established doctrine. Apply the same standard against *LST and the challenger*.
